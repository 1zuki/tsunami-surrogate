\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Domain and physical scope.} Space and time are normalized benchmark quantities. The core data use procedural bathymetry and synthetic sources; the ten GEBCO-derived crops are fully wet, depth-rescaled, and synthetically forced. These experiments assess morphology transfer rather than real-event, site-specific, or operational hazard skill.

\textbf{Reference physics vs. ground truth.} Models are trained against numerical reference solvers rather than physical observations. Discretization, boundary, and interpolation errors persist, and the Boussinesq reference uses a linear, elevation-only formulation without nonlinear wet--dry dynamics. The surrogate evaluation itself uses surface elevation as the predicted state; it does not establish momentum, discharge, energy, or full PDE-residual consistency.

\textbf{Accuracy-diagnostic scope.} The 92-rollout full-horizon study uses six fixed cases, one case per selected bathymetry--source pair, with fixed raster inputs at three nested solver cores and bounded CFL, sponge, boundary, exterior, lake-at-rest, and moving-equilibrium controls. It is therefore not population-representative and does not separately identify spatial, temporal, boundary, damping, and interpolation contributions. The earlier nested-grid study covers twelve smooth variable-bathymetry scenarios on $32 \times 32$, $64 \times 64$, and $128 \times 128$ grids with reflective boundaries, no sponge, and a short final time. The short component-level lake-at-rest check covers three representative bathymetries for 32 steps; the new full-horizon lake-at-rest and moving-equilibrium runs extend the tested scope but do not establish general well-balancedness. The periodic linear-wave analysis uses an analytic linearized reference on $32$--$512$ grids and shows solver-specific empirical behavior rather than a blanket second-order result. The reported targets are linearly interpolated at 50 requested times and then spatially block-mean reduced, so the current metrics do not separately identify temporal interpolation, spatial coarsening, and solver error. Arrival diagnostics are therefore limited to the requested-frame resolution and cannot resolve sub-frame timing. The fixed-prediction reference-resolution analysis includes an additional thirty-case exploratory Hydrostatic study, with one case per bathymetry--source combination and three seeds per model. Equal weighting of combinations differs from test-population weighting. One case per combination cannot resolve within-combination variability; descriptive scenario-bootstrap intervals and model-pair reversals do not quantify population-wide ranking uncertainty. The temporal-output contrast covers only one case and changes integration as well as extraction. The matched GeoClaw comparison retains different boundary treatments and nonuniform changes with refinement. None of these analyses is a formal convergence proof.

\textbf{Statistical replication and benchmarking.} The comparison of all nine direct architectures uses selected seed-$18$ checkpoints, with paired intervals quantifying test-scenario variation rather than training-seed uncertainty. A separate three-seed replication of four leading Hydrostatic models preserves their rank under mean per-scenario relative $L_2$ and quantifies seed spread, but it does not replicate all nine architectures or quantify uncertainty in paired differences between architectures across seeds. The pooled-reference comparison retains three training seeds for its primary matched-training result, while its secondary higher-exposure comparison changes the amount of training exposure and does not isolate that factor. The seven-seed ensemble replicates only the baseline FNO and does not establish architecture-level significance. The offline timing comparison uses different hardware, numerical precision, and implementations for solvers and surrogates; it does not establish an advantage over optimized solvers or measure operational latency.

\textbf{Generalization and uncertainty.} Stress tests assess domain shifts rather than out-of-family invariance. The released dataset contains only trajectories that passed numerical screening. The total number of attempted scenarios and rejection counts by source and bathymetry family are unavailable, so the effect of screening on these distributions cannot be quantified. Deep ensembles remain under-dispersed. Validation-based scaling improves marginal coverage, while simultaneous trajectory bands are broad and still under-cover the high-strength subgroup. Lower mean predicted spread does not identify scenarios with lower relative error in the test cohort. Validation also informed checkpoint selection, so the simultaneous analysis supplies empirical coverage measurements without an independent-calibration or event-level probability guarantee.

\textbf{Data preservation and scope.} The raw and processed datasets and their file inventories are publicly available from the Zenodo records linked in the Data and code availability statement. This study strictly evaluates forward emulation; inverse source reconstruction and real-time data assimilation are excluded.
