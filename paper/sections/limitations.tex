\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The first limitation is the controlled synthetic setting. Procedural bathymetry and source fields provide many repeatable propagation cases, but they do not replace surveyed bathymetry, realistic earthquake rupture models, tides, bottom friction, Coriolis effects, coastal structures, or validation against observed tsunami records. Space and time are expressed in normalized benchmark units rather than mapped to one physical basin and event. The ten-crop real-bathymetry suite remains fully wet and uses synthetic sources, so it tests morphological transfer outside the procedural terrain generator, not readiness for operational warning or site-specific hazard assessment.

The labels are numerical reference solutions, not physical ground truth. Verification supports selected finite-horizon regimes but does not remove discretization, boundary, equation-set, or resolution error. The GeoClaw ablation identifies refinement as the dominant tested sensitivity in three prespecified representative cases, yet the residual remains material and the coupled solver differences prevent attribution to one component. The Boussinesq reference is an elevation-only linear model rather than a nonlinear wet--dry coastal solver. Every reported model error is therefore measured against a stated numerical configuration, not the real ocean.

Common-time extraction aligns output times but neither equates the governing models nor removes interpolation error. Requested states are linearly interpolated only between adjacent natural states and are not fed back into the solvers; their accuracy still depends on bracket width and local temporal curvature. The horizon ends at $0.175$, and the buffer, boundary, and sponge treatment is assessed only for that finite task. Longer propagation may reveal additional reflection, damping, or phase errors.

The replicated direct subset contains only three seeds. It establishes the observed F-FNO--ConvLSTM--FNO--U-Net ordering for those runs but does not provide population confidence intervals or replicate the remaining architectures and stress tests. Training for the seed-$67$ ConvLSTM ended at epoch $124$ because of computational limits; the reported epoch-$118$ checkpoint had already been selected using the predefined validation criterion before test evaluation. The sample-scaling curve also combines seed-$42$ subsets with a seed-$18$ full-data comparator and is not a fitted multi-seed scaling law. Runtime results compare vectorized float64 CPU references with CUDA fp32 inference and exclude loading and setup, so they are implementation measurements rather than operational-solver or end-to-end latency claims.

The transfer and uncertainty diagnostics also have restricted meanings. Metadata-filtered suites are not unseen-family data; strict holdouts are single-seed and change both sample diversity and normalization. Proxy and native resolution tests do not establish arbitrary mesh invariance. Virtual gauges are not observed stations, and arrival eligibility varies strongly across references. The under-dispersed ensemble uses validation-fitted scalar inflation, with coverage marginal over correlated pixels and frames rather than independent events; the high-strength subgroup remains under-covered.

Finally, the versioned Zenodo reproduction package contains the processed common-time datasets, selected checkpoints, evaluation outputs, and checksums. The complete raw trajectories are distributed separately through a mutable public mirror, although they can also be regenerated from the released scripts and archived configurations. The accompanying metadata record the association between checkpoints and processed datasets, but that association is not cryptographically verified for every constituent shard. Full third-party reproduction should therefore verify the released checksums, and long-term raw-data preservation would be strengthened by a future immutable companion deposit. Inverse source reconstruction and real-time data assimilation are also outside the present forward-emulation scope and require separate observations, metrics, uncertainty models, and validation.
