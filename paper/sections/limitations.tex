\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Domain and physical scope.} Space and time are normalized benchmark quantities. The core data use procedural bathymetry and synthetic sources; the ten GEBCO-derived crops are fully wet, depth-rescaled, and synthetically forced. These experiments assess morphology transfer rather than real-event, site-specific, or operational hazard skill.

\textbf{Reference physics vs. ground truth.} Models are trained against numerical reference solvers rather than physical observations. Discretization, boundary, and interpolation errors persist, and the Boussinesq reference uses a linear, elevation-only formulation without nonlinear wet--dry dynamics.

\textbf{Accuracy-diagnostic scope.} The nested-grid study covers twelve smooth variable-bathymetry scenarios on $32 \times 32$, $64 \times 64$, and $128 \times 128$ grids with reflective boundaries, no sponge, and a short final time. The separate periodic linear-wave analysis uses an analytic linearized reference on $32$--$512$ grids and shows solver-specific empirical behavior rather than a blanket second-order result. The requested-time reproduction study covers $120$ selected scenarios and verifies natural-step determinism against stored natural-step records, while retaining small solver-specific output differences from the stored publications. The boundary-flux analysis reports interface flux and a residual normalized by initial domain mass; it does not provide a dimensional or event-scale physical flux budget. None of these analyses is a formal convergence proof.

\textbf{Statistical replication and benchmarking.} The direct architecture comparison is based on selected seed-$18$ checkpoints, with paired intervals quantifying test-scenario variation rather than training-seed uncertainty. The pooled-reference comparison retains three training seeds for its primary matched-training result, while its secondary higher-exposure comparison changes the amount of training exposure and does not isolate that factor. The seven-seed ensemble replicates only the baseline FNO and does not establish architecture-level significance. The study does not include a controlled speed comparison, so no operational latency claim is made.

\textbf{Generalization and uncertainty.} Stress tests assess domain shifts rather than out-of-family invariance. Deep ensembles remain under-dispersed, with scalar variance scaling yielding marginal coverage over space--time pixels rather than calibrated event-level probabilities.

\textbf{Data preservation and scope.} The raw and processed datasets and their release manifests are publicly available from the Zenodo records linked in the Data and code availability statement. This study strictly evaluates forward emulation; inverse source reconstruction and real-time data assimilation are excluded.
