\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The first limitation is the controlled synthetic setting. Procedural bathymetry and source fields provide many repeatable propagation cases, but they do not replace surveyed bathymetry, realistic earthquake rupture models, tides, bottom friction, Coriolis effects, coastal structures, or validation against observed tsunami records. Space and time are expressed in normalized benchmark units rather than mapped to one physical basin and event. The ten-crop real-bathymetry suite remains fully wet and uses synthetic sources, so it tests morphological transfer outside the procedural terrain generator, not readiness for operational warning or site-specific hazard assessment.

The labels are numerical reference solutions, not physical ground truth. Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR are finite-volume shallow-water references with fixed target CFL numbers, state-adaptive time steps, radiation-style boundaries, elapsed-time-consistent sponge damping, and dry-cell safeguards. Internal verification, refinement checks, generation-path tests, and a minimum GeoClaw/Clawpack comparison support the finite-horizon implementation. They do not remove discretization, boundary, equation-set, or resolution error. Moreover, most exploratory production-like GeoClaw comparisons exceeded their descriptive limits even though the analytical and refinement checks passed. The external comparison therefore does not claim pointwise production equivalence, and its selected small-amplitude and refinement cases do not constitute a broad external-validation campaign across the benchmark's bathymetries, amplitudes, boundaries, and dispersive regimes. Every model error in this paper remains an error against a stated numerical reference and configuration, not against the real ocean.

The Boussinesq comparison has an especially limited interpretation. It is an elevation-only linear weakly dispersive model with a variable-depth acceleration solve, finite-depth safeguards, sponge damping, and a disabled production filter. It omits the nonlinear and wet--dry capabilities expected of a coastal production Boussinesq solver. The constant-depth dispersion test and selected independent comparisons support its use as an exploratory label family, not as an automatic fidelity upgrade over the shallow-water references.

Common-time extraction aligns the reported outputs but does not force identical internal steps. The shallow-water steps adapt to the evolving state, while the Boussinesq step is constant within each fixed-bathymetry rollout. Requested states are linearly interpolated only between the adjacent natural states that bracket each requested time. This avoids extrapolation and does not feed interpolants back into the solver, but it still introduces an extraction approximation whose size depends on the natural bracket width and local temporal curvature. The shared time vector also does not make the three equation sets physically equivalent. The horizon ends at $0.175$ and the buffer/sponge treatment is assessed only for this finite task; longer propagation may expose additional reflection or damping effects.

The direct architecture comparison is not fully replicated. The eleven model--reference configurations use selected single-seed checkpoints, and the seven additional seeds cover only the Hydrostatic FNO. Their small spread supports stability for that one configuration but cannot attach seed-level uncertainty to the F-FNO-versus-ConvLSTM ranking or to every baseline. Likewise, the sample-scaling curve uses seed $42$ for $N = 100$ through $5{,}000$, while the $10{,}000$ comparator uses seed $18$. It is a useful monotone diagnostic, not a fitted multi-seed scaling law.

The runtime comparison is implementation-level. The denominator is the exact common-time vectorized float64 CPU NumPy/SciPy rollout, while model inference uses CUDA fp32 on a laptop GPU at batch size one. Warm-up and repeated measurements reduce transient timing effects, but the hardware, language, precision, and level of optimization differ. Loading and setup are excluded from the solver denominator. The reported acceleration is therefore a reproducible comparison for these implementations, not a comparison with compiled, GPU, adaptive-mesh, or operational tsunami solvers and not an end-to-end warning-system latency.

The distribution-shift and resolution results depend on how their datasets are constructed. Metadata-filtered suites are subgroups of the ordinary test distribution, not unseen-family data. The four strict holdouts are stronger because one family is removed before normalization and training, but each is a single-seed experiment and changes both sample diversity and normalization. Proxy resolution transfer resizes existing arrays, whereas the native MUSCL-HR matrix uses only $700/150/150$ scenarios per grid and an explicit normalization bridge. Neither design establishes arbitrary mesh invariance, asymptotic resolution behavior, or transfer to meshes and geometries not represented here.

The gauge and uncertainty metrics also have restricted meanings. Virtual gauges lie on the normalized interior grid and do not correspond to observed stations. Arrival error is defined only for target gauges that cross a target-only threshold after the first requested time; eligibility varies substantially across references, so arrival statistics are not directly rankable without their counts. The seven-member Gaussian ensemble is under-dispersed, and scalar variance inflation is fitted post hoc on validation elements. Its coverage is marginal over strongly correlated pixels and frames, not over independent events, and the high-strength subgroup remains under-covered.

Finally, the versioned Zenodo reproduction package contains the processed common-time datasets, selected checkpoints, evaluation evidence, and checksums, but the complete eta-primary raw publications are distributed separately through a mutable public mirror rather than a second immutable deposit. They can also be regenerated from the released scripts and frozen configurations. The retained configurations and manifests identify the processed datasets used by the checkpoints, but the principal association is not independently authenticated against the content hash of every constituent shard. Full third-party reproduction should therefore verify the released checksums rather than relying on checkpoint metadata alone, and long-term raw-data preservation would be strengthened by a future immutable companion deposit. Inverse source reconstruction and real-time data assimilation are also outside the present forward-emulation scope and require separate observations, metrics, uncertainty models, and validation.
