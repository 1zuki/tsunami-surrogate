\begin{abstract}
Numerical solvers used to train PDE surrogates are often treated as neutral label generators, although discretization, governing equations, and output timing can alter apparent model performance. We formulate and evaluate a reference-aware protocol for tsunami-like wave emulation in which identical bathymetry--source scenarios are advanced by Hydrostatic, MUSCL-HR, and linear weakly dispersive Boussinesq references. Each solver retains its natural time steps but publishes 50 adjacent-step interpolants on one requested-time vector, enabling same-time solver and surrogate comparisons. The finite-horizon label contract is supported by 86 internal analytical/numerical cases, 90 generation-path cases with three deterministic replay checks, and an independent 363-case sensitivity set. Matched GeoClaw/Clawpack analytical and refinement checks support selected regimes, while broader production-like agreement remains limited. On 2,500 held-out scenarios, the Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR RMSE gap ($0.00508$) is $1.6$ times the Hydrostatic FNO error and $2.1$ times the F-FNO error; the shallow-water--Boussinesq gaps near $0.045$ exceed the same-reference Boussinesq FNO error by more than five times. Cross-reference discrepancy ratios remain near one, showing that a surrogate can move closer to a second numerical reference without becoming more physically accurate. Comparisons across established architectures, resolution transfer, morphology shift, and ensemble uncertainty serve as stress tests of the protocol rather than claims of a new neural architecture. The results show that PDE-surrogate conclusions are conditional on an explicit numerical reference and temporal output contract; the synthetic, finite-horizon setting does not establish operational tsunami forecasting or event-scale physical validation.
\end{abstract}
