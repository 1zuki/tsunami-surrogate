\begin{abstract}
Numerical references can change apparent PDE-surrogate performance through their equations, discretization, and output timing. We evaluate a reference-aware shallow-water benchmark using identical bathymetry--source scenarios with Hydrostatic, MUSCL-HR, and a study-defined linear weakly dispersive Boussinesq-type reference, sampled at $50$ shared times through $420$. A $92$-rollout study finds contracting adjacent-grid differences across six fixed cases, alongside material boundary and damping sensitivity. Matched refinement improves Hydrostatic agreement with GeoClaw in all three cases, while MUSCL-HR agreement changes nonuniformly. On $2{,}500$ held-out scenarios, global inter-reference RMSEs range from $0.00171$ to $0.00374$ in benchmark units. On $30$ additional family-balanced scenarios, refining the Hydrostatic evaluation reference increases observed mean relative errors by $2.82\%$--$6.60\%$ with predictions held fixed, and exchanges the aggregate F-FNO and U-FNO ordering. In a three-seed pooled comparison, supplying solver identity reduces target-specific FNO errors. A seven-member ensemble remains unreliable for difficult cases: at nominal $90\%$, empirical simultaneous trajectory coverage is $88.72\%$ overall and $67.80\%$ for high-strength sources. These results support evaluating surrogate error together with numerical-reference sensitivity and subgroup reliability. The fixed-raster, synthetic finite-horizon experiments do not establish formal convergence, event-scale physical accuracy, or operational forecasting skill.
\end{abstract}
