\begin{abstract}
We study neural-operator surrogates for tsunami-like wave propagation in a controlled synthetic benchmark based on two-dimensional shallow-water and weakly dispersive reference simulations. Each scenario pairs a procedurally generated bathymetry field with a synthetic initial displacement source, and the same scenario can be advanced by multiple numerical references to separate surrogate error from solver-fidelity effects. The primary experiments evaluate a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) against convolutional baselines under matched train/test protocols. We focus on solver fidelity, inference speedup, robustness under distribution shift via metadata-filtered source, bathymetry, and source-strength shift diagnostics, cross-resolution transfer from coarse to finer grids, and ensemble-based uncertainty quality. We further study transfer from the synthetic training distribution to real bathymetric geometries, where a model trained only on procedural terrain is evaluated on real-world bathymetry, as a test of whether the surrogate has learned propagation behaviour rather than memorised the synthetic generator. The benchmark is designed for research on fast forward emulation of solver-generated wavefields, not for operational tsunami warning or site-specific hazard assessment.
\end{abstract}
