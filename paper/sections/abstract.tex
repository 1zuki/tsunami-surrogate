\begin{abstract}
We study fast neural-operator surrogates for tsunami-like wave propagation in a controlled synthetic setting based on two-dimensional shallow-water simulations. Our pipeline generates diverse bathymetry and source families, and uses the resulting numerical trajectories as reference data for supervised learning. We evaluate a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) as the primary surrogate model and compare it against convolutional baselines under matched training and evaluation protocols. The experiments focus on three questions: fidelity to the numerical solver, inference speedup, and robustness under distribution shift, including unseen bathymetry families and cross-resolution transfer from coarse to finer grids. Our results are intended to assess whether operator-learning models can provide meaningful acceleration for tsunami surrogate modeling while preserving acceptable predictive accuracy in a research setting. We emphasize that this work targets controlled surrogate modeling rather than operational early-warning deployment, including unseen bathymetry families and cross-resolution transfer from coarse to finer grids, with an additional uncertainty analysis of predictive confidence. All the code for this paper can be found here \text{https://github.com/1zuki/tsunami-surrogate}
\end{abstract}
