\begin{abstract}
Numerical solvers used to train PDE surrogates are often treated as neutral label generators, although discretization, governing equations, and output timing can alter apparent model performance. We formulate and evaluate a reference-aware protocol for tsunami-like wave emulation in which identical bathymetry--source scenarios are advanced by Hydrostatic, MUSCL-HR, and linear weakly dispersive Boussinesq references. Each solver retains its natural time steps while its outputs are interpolated to 50 shared requested times, enabling same-time separation of surrogate error from reference-to-reference discrepancy. Analytical, numerical, repeatability, and independent sensitivity tests support the finite-horizon implementation. Comparisons with GeoClaw/Clawpack agree within specified criteria for analytical-wave and prescribed refinement tests, but three representative finite-horizon shallow-water cases retain material discrepancies. MUSCL-HR is consistently closer than Hydrostatic, and further refinement reduces but does not eliminate the disagreement. On 2,500 held-out scenarios, the Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR discrepancy in global RMSE is $1.6$ and $2.1$ times the same-reference errors of the Hydrostatic FNO and F-FNO, respectively, while the shallow-water--Boussinesq discrepancies exceed the same-reference Boussinesq FNO error by more than five times. Across seeds $18$, $36$, and $67$, the Hydrostatic mean per-scenario relative-$L_2$ ordering remains F-FNO, ConvLSTM, FNO, then U-Net. Cross-reference ratios remain above one for both shallow-water directions, whereas the cross-family effects remain near equality and are not uniformly seed-robust. Thus closeness to a second numerical reference does not establish physical superiority. The results show that PDE-surrogate conclusions are conditional on an explicit numerical reference and temporal-output protocol; the synthetic, finite-horizon setting does not establish operational tsunami forecasting or event-scale physical validation.
\end{abstract}
