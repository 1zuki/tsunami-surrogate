\begin{abstract}
We study neural-operator surrogates for tsunami-like wave propagation in a controlled synthetic benchmark based on two-dimensional shallow-water and weakly dispersive reference simulations. Each scenario pairs a procedurally generated bathymetry field with a synthetic initial displacement source, and the same scenario can be advanced by multiple numerical references to separate surrogate error from solver-fidelity effects. Under matched train/test protocols, the primary experiments evaluate FNO and factorized FNO (FFNO) surrogates against convolutional and operator baselines while measuring solver fidelity, implementation-level inference speedup, distribution-shift behavior through metadata-filtered and strict held-out-family diagnostics, cross-resolution transfer, physics diagnostics, and ensemble uncertainty with $M = 7$ members. We also test whether models trained on the synthetic distribution can be applied beyond the procedural terrain generator by evaluating direct and seeded windowed rollouts on a fully wet, 10-crop real-bathymetry morphology suite under synthetic forcing. These transfer results are interpreted conservatively: they probe morphology shift under controlled forcing, not real-event validation, operational tsunami warning, or site-specific hazard assessment.
\end{abstract}
