\begin{abstract}
How much does the numerical reference change the apparent performance of a neural surrogate for tsunami-like wave propagation? We study this question in a controlled synthetic benchmark in which identical bathymetry--source scenarios are advanced by Hydrostatic, MUSCL-HR, and weakly dispersive Boussinesq references. A common-time contract publishes all references at the same 50 requested times from $0.0035$ to $0.175$ on a central $64 \times 64$ crop of a $96 \times 96$ computation, ensuring that cross-reference comparisons use one shared temporal grid. In the single-seed direct comparisons, F-FNO and ConvLSTM give the lowest Hydrostatic relative $L_2$ errors ($0.190$ and $0.211$), followed by the base FNO ($0.244$). The common-time Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR gap is much smaller than either shallow-water--Boussinesq gap, but cross-reference discrepancy ratios remain close to one in several directions; this means the surrogate sometimes shifts slightly toward another numerical reference, not that it becomes physically more accurate. Seeded window models reduce long-horizon error but solve an easier conditional task. On ten fully wet real-bathymetry crops under synthetic forcing, direct F-FNO reaches relative $L_2 = 0.218$, while a wet--dry coastline stress case fails near unity. A seven-member FNO ensemble tracks error only moderately and under-covers before validation-only variance inflation. These results support neural surrogates as fast emulators of stated numerical references, while limiting the claim to a synthetic, finite-horizon benchmark rather than operational tsunami forecasting.
\end{abstract}
