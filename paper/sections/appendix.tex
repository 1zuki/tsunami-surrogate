\subsection{Configuration details}
\label{app:conf-details}

The experiments share a single base training configuration, with per-model overrides layered on top. Table~\ref{tab:app-config} lists the settings that are referenced only indirectly in the main text. \subsection{Additional solver diagnostics}
\label{app:solver-diag}

Beyond the dataset-level quality policy of Section~\ref{sec:data-generation}, both shallow-water references are checked against two standard finite-volume sanity properties in the test suite. The first is the lake-at-rest (well-balanced) property: starting from a flat free surface $\eta_0 = 0$ over non-flat fully submerged bathymetry with zero initial momentum, a well-balanced scheme must keep the surface flat and the momentum zero rather than spuriously generating flow at the bathymetry gradients. Both the hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR solvers hold this to a tight tolerance over a short rollout: the maximum free-surface drift stays below $10 ^ {-8}$ and the maximum momentum component below $10 ^ {-7}$ in normalized units. The second is a dynamic-pulse stability check: a small Gaussian surface perturbation is advanced over flat, sloped, and multi-Gaussian bathymetry under periodic and open boundaries, and the rollout must stay finite, keep depth nonnegative (to within $10 ^ {-8}$), respect the velocity safeguard, and still produce a non-trivial propagating signal. The MUSCL-HR solver passes these across the tested grids ($32 ^ 2$ and $64 ^ 2$) and boundary conditions. These are sanity checks, not convergence-order measurements, external-solver validations, observed-event validations, or production-code validations; they are guardrails confirming that the references preserve the trivial steady state and remain stable on dynamic inputs, which is what justifies treating their outputs as clean supervised-learning labels.

\paragraph{Boussinesq dispersion check.}
As an appendix-only sensitivity check for the weakly dispersive reference, we propagated small-amplitude sinusoidal waves on a $256 \times 32$ constant-depth grid with periodic boundaries, no sponge, and no filter. The full diagnostic uses modes $1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32$, final time $1.5$, and fixed step size $\Delta t = 1.25 \times 10 ^ {-4}$. The initial condition uses the expected discrete frequency for a right-going Fourier mode, and the measured frequency is recovered from the unwrapped phase of the corresponding Fourier coefficient. The expected trend is the implemented discrete relation
\[
    \omega ^ 2 =
    \frac{g H k_d ^ 2}{1 + \alpha_B H ^ 2 k_d ^ 2},
    \qquad
    k_d = \frac{2 \sin(k \Delta x / 2)}{\Delta x}.
\]

Figure~\ref{fig:app-bouss-dispersion} shows the measured phase speeds for $\alpha_B = 0$ and $\alpha_B = 1 / 3$. With $\alpha_B = 0$, the phase speed follows the non-dispersive shallow-water value modified only by the discrete wavenumber. With $\alpha_B = 1 / 3$, the higher modes slow sharply, matching the implemented weakly dispersive trend. The maximum measured phase-speed relative error is $2.45 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ for $\alpha_B = 0$ and $1.91 \times 10 ^ {-8}$ for $\alpha_B = 1 / 3$ in this diagnostic.

\paragraph{Reliability-curve diagnostic and calibration status.}
Figure~\ref{fig:app-ensemble-reliability} replots the full regenerated ensemble coverage summaries as a coverage-versus-nominal reliability curve. We fit a single post-hoc marginal scale correction, $\sigma ^ 2 \mapsto \gamma \sigma ^ 2$, on validation labels only, with $\gamma = 3.359364$ and corresponding standard-deviation scale $1.832857$, then apply it unchanged to the test and OOD suites. Raw intervals under-cover; the scalar correction improves marginal coverage on the in-distribution test split and some OOD suites, while the hardest high source-strength tail remains under-covered. This is a post-hoc scalar correction, not operational risk calibration.

\subsection{Sample-scaling diagnostic}
\label{app:sample-scaling}

We isolate sample-count effects by training the same hydrostatic FNO on nested random subsets of the $10{,}000$-sample pool, with optimizer, schedule, early stopping, batch size, and test split ($N = 2500$) fixed. Figures~\ref{fig:sample-scaling}--\ref{fig:sample-scaling-metrics} plot relative $L_2$ and companion metrics; Table~\ref{tab:sample-scaling} gives the values.

Relative $L_2$ drops monotonically from $0.586$ at $N = 100$ to $0.235$ at $N = 10{,}000$, with largest gains below $N = 1{,}000$ and diminishing returns past $N = 2{,}500$. This single-seed diagnostic is not a converged scaling law; the two smallest subsets ($N \le 250$) hit the $256$-epoch cap but are validation-flat to within $10^{-3}$ over the final $50$ epochs, and larger subsets early-stop.
