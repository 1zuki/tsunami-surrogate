\subsection{Reproduction of requested-time outputs from the same dataset}
\label{app:requested-time-reproduction}

This analysis uses the same dataset as the main evaluation; it does not create a second scenario population. The raw data records were examined alongside their requested-output publications and paired 128-cell input caches. All $2{,}500$ scenario records were complete across the three references, and the shared inputs, family labels, source strengths, requested times, finite-state flags, and quality records matched across solvers. The primary selection contains $120$ scenarios with four cases per bathymetry--source family cell.

For these same $120$ scenarios, the three reference solvers were rerun from the paired 128-cell inputs with the stated $192 \times 192$ buffered settings: a $128 \times 128$ solver core, a 32-cell buffer, requested times $8.4$--$420$, target CFL values $0.1125$, $0.225$, and $0.35$, radiation boundaries for Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR, an open boundary for Boussinesq, and the elapsed-time-consistent cosine sponge. This rerun regenerates the requested-time outputs; it does not alter the dataset used for training or evaluation. All $360$ solver--scenario computations remained finite. Every natural-step count matched the stored natural-step records, and the maximum absolute difference between regenerated and archived natural-step histories was $1.39\times10^{-16}$ for Hydrostatic, $6.11\times10^{-16}$ for MUSCL-HR, and zero for Boussinesq.

\subsection{Analytic periodic linear-wave accuracy analysis}
\label{app:periodic-linear-convergence}

The nested-grid analysis compares numerical solutions with one another. To provide a linearized reference, we propagated a small-amplitude right-going sinusoid on a constant-depth periodic domain with the Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR implementations used in this study. The grid sequence was $32$, $64$, $128$, $256$, and $512$ cells in the propagation direction, with a fixed transverse width of $32$ cells, final time $0.5$, and target CFL values $0.1125$, $0.225$, and $0.45$. The analytic phase reference uses the linear shallow-water wave speed. All $30$ simulations remained finite. The finest-grid relative-$L_2$ error was $0.0334$--$0.0526$ for Hydrostatic and $0.007394$--$0.007396$ for MUSCL-HR across the three CFL values.

Boundary, sponge, and domain changes produce small shifts, while the nested refinement substantially reduces the discrepancy for both shallow-water references. The residual depends jointly on solver formulation, boundary treatment, and resolution; these results do not establish pointwise equivalence, formal convergence, general boundary independence, or event-scale validity.

\paragraph{Boussinesq dispersion analysis.}
Small-amplitude periodic sinusoidal waves are propagated on a $256 \times 32$ constant-depth grid for modes $1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32$ at $\Delta t = 1.25 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ and final time $1.5$. Fourier phase tracking is compared with the discrete relation
\begin{equation}
    \omega ^ 2 =
    \frac{g H k_d ^ 2}{1 + \alpha_B H ^ 2 k_d ^ 2},
    \qquad
    k_d = \frac{2 \sin(k \Delta x / 2)}{\Delta x}.
\end{equation}
The $\alpha_B = 0$ branch approaches the non-dispersive shallow-water relation, while $\alpha_B = 1/3$ slows higher modes according to the weakly dispersive operator used here. Figure~\ref{fig:app-bouss-dispersion} shows the phase-speed comparison, and Table~\ref{tab:app-bouss-dispersion} gives the maximum errors and linear-solver status. This is a local numerical analysis, not external-solver or event validation.
