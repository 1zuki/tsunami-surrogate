\subsection{Configuration details}
\label{app:conf-details}
The experiments share a single base training configuration, with per-model overrides layered on top. Table~\ref{tab:app-config} lists the settings that are referenced only indirectly in the main text. The main hydrostatic FNO widens the base architecture (width $48$, depth $6$, $14$ spectral modes per axis against the base $32$/$4$/$12$) and replaces the plain MSE objective with a horizon-weighted MSE that up-weights late frames, together with cosine learning-rate decay, gradient clipping, and a longer early-stopping patience. The convolutional baselines and the cross-resolution and windowed variants reuse this base and change only the fields noted in their respective sections.

\subsection{Additional solver diagnostics}
\label{app:solver-diag}
Beyond the dataset-level quality policy of Section~\ref{sec:data-generation}, both shallow-water references are checked against two standard finite-volume sanity properties in the test suite. The first is the lake-at-rest (well-balanced) property: starting from a flat free surface $\eta_0 = 0$ over non-flat fully submerged bathymetry with zero initial momentum, a well-balanced scheme must keep the surface flat and the momentum zero rather than spuriously generating flow at the bathymetry gradients. Both the hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR solvers hold this to a tight tolerance over a short rollout: the maximum free-surface drift stays below $10 ^ {-8}$ and the maximum momentum component below $10 ^ {-7}$ in normalized units. The second is a dynamic-pulse stability check: a small Gaussian surface perturbation is advanced over flat, sloped, and multi-Gaussian bathymetry under periodic and open boundaries, and the rollout must stay finite, keep depth nonnegative (to within $10 ^ {-8}$), respect the velocity safeguard, and still produce a non-trivial propagating signal. The MUSCL-HR solver passes these across the tested grids ($32 ^ 2$ and $64 ^ 2$) and boundary conditions. These are not convergence-order measurements; they are guardrails confirming that the references preserve the trivial steady state and remain stable on dynamic inputs, which is what justifies treating their outputs as clean supervised-learning labels.

\subsection{Sample-scaling diagnostic}
\label{app:sample-scaling}
To characterize how the hydrostatic FNO's accuracy depends on the amount of training data, we train the same architecture on nested random subsets of the $10{,}000$-sample training pool, holding the optimizer, learning-rate schedule, early-stopping criterion, and the held-out test split ($N = 2500$) fixed across runs. Each subset is materialized into a flat in-memory dataset so that every run trains with the same constant batch size, removing batch size as a confound. Figure~\ref{fig:sample-scaling} plots the resulting test relative $L_2$ against training-set size on a log-scaled horizontal axis, and Figure~\ref{fig:sample-scaling-metrics} shows the companion MAE, RMSE, and relative $L_2$ curves. Table~\ref{tab:sample-scaling} reports the underlying numbers.

The curve is monotone and smooth: relative $L_2$ falls from $0.586$ at $N = 100$ to $0.235$ at the full $N = 10{,}000$, with the steepest gains below $N = 1{,}000$ and clear diminishing returns past $N = 2{,}500$. We label this a single-seed diagnostic rather than a converged scaling law, since each point is one training run and we do not estimate run-to-run variance. The two smallest subsets ($N \le 250$) train to the $256$-epoch cap, but their validation relative $L_2$ is flat to within $10 ^ {-3}$ over the final $50$ epochs, indicating data-limited rather than budget-limited convergence; the larger subsets early-stop before the cap.

Both coastline rows use the same GEBCO-derived Japan-Trench-region crop (internal tag \texttt{gebco\_japan\_coast}), covering lon $138^\circ$--$144^\circ$E and lat $30 ^ \circ$--$36 ^ \circ$N, resampled to $64\times64$ under the identical Okada-like synthetic source (strength $0.826$, seed $367$) as the main offshore diagnostic; only the depth-range rescale differs. The crop is taken from the GEBCO 2026 Global grid. The two coastline rows separate morphology from wet-dry shift. When the coastline geometry is linearly rescaled into the fully wet range $[-10,\,-0.75]$, both models remain usable: the direct FNO gives rel-$L_2=0.245$ and Window-FNO-5 gives rel-$L_2=0.065$. When the same coastline crop keeps positive topography in the $[-10,\,5]$ range, the reference rollout triggers a small quality warning (maximum $|\eta|=5.0135$ against the configured limit $5.0$), and both models rise to rel-$L_2\approx1.0$. We therefore interpret the stress failure mainly as a dry-land/positive-topography distribution shift, not as evidence that coastline geometry alone breaks the surrogate.
