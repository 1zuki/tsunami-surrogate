\subsection{Full-horizon numerical sensitivity study}
\label{app:full-horizon-sensitivity}

The $92$ rollouts cover six nested-grid/CFL cases, three boundary-control cases, five stationary cases with both solvers, and two moving-equilibrium resolutions with both solvers. Section~\ref{subsec:res-dataset} and Figure~\ref{fig:full-horizon-numerical-study} give the detailed results. These fixed-raster comparisons measure combined numerical sensitivities rather than formal convergence or physical accuracy.

\subsection{Analytic periodic linear-wave accuracy analysis}
\label{app:periodic-linear-convergence}

The final step was shortened to reach $0.5$ exactly. All $30$ runs remained finite; Table~\ref{tab:app-periodic-linear-convergence} compares them with the linear shallow-water phase reference.

    \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The solvers advance nonlinear equations while the reference is linearized, so the error includes finite-amplitude effects and harmonic generation. The MUSCL-HR plateau cannot be attributed uniquely to discretization, and fixed-grid CFL comparisons do not establish temporal order.

At CFL $0.1125$, an amplitude check repeated the $128$, $256$, and $512$ grids at initial amplitudes $10 ^ {-4}$ and $10 ^ {-5}$, alongside the original $10 ^ {-3}$ runs. The MUSCL-HR finest-grid relative error decreased from $0.007394$ to $0.001865$ and $0.001722$; Hydrostatic changed from $0.05259$ to $0.05218$ and $0.05217$. Amplitude reduction therefore removes part of the discrepancy but leaves a residual; it does not isolate spatial error or establish spatial order.

On the continental/Gaussian case, a separate $128$-cell-core check at common CFL $0.1125$ shortened steps to land on all 50 output times. Relative trajectory differences from natural-step interpolation were $0.0134\%$ for Hydrostatic and $0.00182\%$ for MUSCL-HR. This one-case contrast changes time integration as well as output extraction and does not isolate interpolation error or establish its size across the benchmark.

\subsection{Boussinesq dispersion analysis}
\label{app:boussinesq-dispersion}
Small-amplitude periodic sinusoidal waves are propagated on a $256 \times 32$ constant-depth grid for modes $1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32$ at $\Delta t = 1.25 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ and final time $1.5$. Fourier phase tracking is compared with the discrete relation
\begin{equation}
    \omega ^ 2 =
    \frac{g H k_d ^ 2}{1 + \alpha_B H ^ 2 k_d ^ 2},
    \qquad
    k_d = \frac{2 \sin(k \Delta x / 2)}{\Delta x}.
\end{equation}
The $\alpha_B=0$ branch is non-dispersive; $\alpha_B=1/3$ slows higher modes. Figure~\ref{fig:app-bouss-dispersion} and Table~\ref{tab:app-bouss-dispersion} report phase speeds and solver status. This is a local numerical check rather than external or event validation.
