\subsection{Configuration details}
\label{app:conf-details}

The experiments share a single base training configuration, with per-model overrides layered on top. Table~\ref{tab:app-config} lists the settings that are referenced only indirectly in the main text. The main hydrostatic FNO widens the base architecture (width $48$, depth $6$, $14$ spectral modes per axis against the base $32$/$4$/$12$) and replaces the plain MSE objective with a horizon-weighted MSE that up-weights late frames, together with cosine learning-rate decay, gradient clipping, and a longer early-stopping patience. The convolutional baselines and the cross-resolution and windowed variants reuse this base and change only the fields noted in their respective sections.

\subsection{Additional solver diagnostics}
\label{app:solver-diag}

Beyond the dataset-level quality policy of Section~\ref{sec:data-generation}, both shallow-water references are checked against two standard finite-volume sanity properties in the test suite. The first is the lake-at-rest (well-balanced) property: starting from a flat free surface $\eta_0 = 0$ over non-flat fully submerged bathymetry with zero initial momentum, a well-balanced scheme must keep the surface flat and the momentum zero rather than spuriously generating flow at the bathymetry gradients. Both the hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR solvers hold this to a tight tolerance over a short rollout: the maximum free-surface drift stays below $10 ^ {-8}$ and the maximum momentum component below $10 ^ {-7}$ in normalized units. The second is a dynamic-pulse stability check: a small Gaussian surface perturbation is advanced over flat, sloped, and multi-Gaussian bathymetry under periodic and open boundaries, and the rollout must stay finite, keep depth nonnegative (to within $10 ^ {-8}$), respect the velocity safeguard, and still produce a non-trivial propagating signal. The MUSCL-HR solver passes these across the tested grids ($32 ^ 2$ and $64 ^ 2$) and boundary conditions. These are not convergence-order measurements; they are guardrails confirming that the references preserve the trivial steady state and remain stable on dynamic inputs, which is what justifies treating their outputs as clean supervised-learning labels.

\subsection{Sample-scaling diagnostic}
\label{app:sample-scaling}

We isolate sample-count effects by training the same hydrostatic FNO on nested random subsets of the $10{,}000$-sample pool, with optimizer, schedule, early stopping, batch size, and test split ($N = 2500$) fixed. Figures~\ref{fig:sample-scaling}--\ref{fig:sample-scaling-metrics} plot relative $L_2$ and companion metrics; Table~\ref{tab:sample-scaling} gives the values.

Relative $L_2$ drops monotonically from $0.586$ at $N = 100$ to $0.235$ at $N = 10{,}000$, with largest gains below $N = 1{,}000$ and diminishing returns past $N = 2{,}500$. This single-seed diagnostic is not a converged scaling law; the two smallest subsets ($N \le 250$) hit the $256$-epoch cap but are validation-flat to within $10^{-3}$ over the final $50$ epochs, and larger subsets early-stop.

When the coastline geometry is linearly rescaled into the fully wet range $[-10,\, -0.75]$, all four model variants remain usable. We therefore interpret the stress failure mainly as a dry-land/positive-topography distribution shift, not as evidence that coastline geometry alone breaks the surrogate.
