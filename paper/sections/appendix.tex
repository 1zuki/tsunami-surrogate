Both coastline rows use the same GEBCO-derived Japan-Trench-region crop (internal tag \texttt{gebco\_japan\_coast}), covering lon $138^\circ$--$144^\circ$E and lat $30 ^ \circ$--$36 ^ \circ$N, resampled to $64\times64$ under the identical Okada-like synthetic source (strength $0.826$, seed $367$) as the main offshore diagnostic; only the depth-range rescale differs. The crop is taken from the GEBCO 2026 Global grid. The two coastline rows separate morphology from wet-dry shift. When the coastline geometry is linearly rescaled into the fully wet range $[-10,\,-0.75]$, both models remain usable: the direct FNO gives rel-$L_2=0.245$ and Window-FNO-5 gives rel-$L_2=0.063$. When the same coastline crop keeps positive topography in the $[-10,\,5]$ range, the reference rollout triggers a small quality warning (maximum $|\eta|=5.0135$ against the configured limit $5.0$), and both models rise to rel-$L_2\approx1.0$. We therefore interpret the stress failure mainly as a dry-land/positive-topography distribution shift, not as evidence that coastline geometry alone breaks the surrogate.
