\section{Introduction}
\setlength{\parskip}{0.5em}

Neural PDE surrogates are evaluated against trajectories generated by numerical solvers \citep{kovachki2023neuraloperator,takamoto2022pdebench}. Those trajectories are not neutral ground truth: the governing equation, spatial discretization, time integration, boundary treatment, and output schedule define the operator that the network is asked to emulate \citep{leveque2002finite,toro2009riemann,koehler2026emulator}. When reference-to-reference discrepancy is comparable to the surrogate error, an apparent architecture improvement can instead reflect which numerical target was selected and how its outputs were sampled \citep{koehler2026emulator}.

Tsunami-like shallow-water propagation provides a demanding computational-fluid setting for this problem. Physics-based rollouts become expensive when repeated across many source scenarios, bathymetric fields, numerical resolutions, or uncertainty-analysis runs, which motivates fast emulators \citep{leveque2011tsunami,berger2011geoclaw,wojcik2025aitsunami,surrogatePiso2026}. At the same time, wave phase, numerical dissipation, boundary treatment, and dispersive assumptions can make the numerical reference a material part of the learning problem rather than a passive source of labels \citep{leveque2002finite,berger2011geoclaw,shi2012boussinesq}.

The benchmark is intended as a computational-fluid surrogate study rather than a site-specific tsunami model. Recent fluid-dynamics surrogate work treats learned models as components of numerical solvers, reduced-order workflows, and uncertainty analyses, which makes solver coupling, regime coverage, and calibration part of the evaluation problem \citep{surrogatePiso2026,streetcanyon2026,romAdapt2026,fluidUQ2026}.

We cast forward emulation as an operator-learning task: given bathymetry and an initial free-surface disturbance, the model predicts a future trajectory generated by a stated numerical reference. Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) provide the primary controlled comparison \citep{li2021fno}. Factorized FNO, U-FNO, WNO, CNN, U-Net, and ConvLSTM configurations probe different global, local, multiscale, and recurrent inductive biases \citep{tran2023ffno,wen2022ufno,tripura2023wno,lecun1998gradient,ronneberger2015unet,shi2015convlstm}. These established architectures are instruments for examining the evaluation framework; the contribution is not a new neural architecture.

Multi-reference evaluation additionally requires a shared temporal meaning. Equal tensor shapes do not imply equal physical states if frames are selected by solver-step index while adaptive step sizes differ. We therefore let every reference retain its natural internal steps but publish adjacent-step interpolants on one requested-time vector. This common-time procedure permits paired comparisons without forcing the solvers onto an identical internal step sequence.

Common-time alignment is a necessary methodological practice rather than a novel numerical contribution. The contribution is the controlled empirical comparison that it enables: the same scenarios and neural predictions can receive different accuracy scores and case-level model orderings when the numerical reference changes. Shared inputs and output times make that comparison interpretable; refinement and independent-code checks bound what it says about numerical accuracy.

Time alignment does not make one reference ground truth. Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR approximate the depth-averaged shallow-water equations with different discretizations, while the study-defined Boussinesq-type comparison introduces a linear weakly dispersive correction \citep{audusse2004hydrostatic,clain2016secondorder,shi2012boussinesq}. Their pairwise gaps combine equation-set and numerical effects. We therefore separate same-reference surrogate error, raw reference-to-reference discrepancy, and cross-reference surrogate discrepancy. A surrogate that lies closer to a second solver in one norm has not thereby become more physically accurate \citep{koehler2026emulator}.

The central question is consequently computational rather than operational: after the references are aligned to the same requested times, how does changing the numerical reference alter measured surrogate accuracy and model comparisons? Distribution shift, resolution transfer, predictive uncertainty, and transfer to real bathymetric morphology provide supporting stress tests within the same finite-horizon setting.

\noindent
The main contributions of this study are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=3.6em]
    \item a matched multi-reference shallow-water benchmark that separates same-reference surrogate error, raw reference gaps, and cross-reference discrepancy on identical scenarios and 50 shared requested times,
    \item a fixed-prediction experiment on $30$ additional family-balanced scenarios showing that Hydrostatic reference refinement increases observed mean relative error by $2.82\%$--$6.60\%$ and changes the aggregate F-FNO/U-FNO ordering, alongside the original six-case, 92-rollout sensitivity study and independent-code comparisons that delimit the numerical interpretation,
    \item complementary pooled-reference and uncertainty experiments showing how solver identity affects target-specific agreement and how near-nominal aggregate coverage can coexist with substantial subgroup undercoverage. Architecture, resolution-transfer, and bathymetric-morphology comparisons characterize the scope of these findings.
\end{enumerate}
