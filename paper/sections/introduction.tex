\section{Introduction}
\setlength{\parskip}{0.5em}

Neural PDE surrogates are evaluated against trajectories generated by numerical solvers \citep{kovachki2023neuraloperator,takamoto2022pdebench}. Those trajectories are not neutral ground truth: the governing equation, spatial discretization, time integration, boundary treatment, and output schedule define the operator that the network is asked to emulate \citep{leveque2002finite,toro2009riemann,koehler2026emulator}. When reference-to-reference discrepancy is comparable to the surrogate error, an apparent architecture improvement can instead reflect which numerical target was selected and how its outputs were sampled \citep{koehler2026emulator}.

Tsunami-like wave propagation provides a demanding setting for this problem. Physics-based rollouts become expensive when repeated across many source scenarios, bathymetric fields, numerical resolutions, or uncertainty-analysis runs, which motivates fast emulators \citep{leveque2011tsunami,berger2011geoclaw,wojcik2025aitsunami}. At the same time, wave phase, numerical dissipation, boundary treatment, and dispersive assumptions can make the numerical reference a material part of the learning problem rather than a passive source of labels \citep{leveque2002finite,berger2011geoclaw,shi2012boussinesq}.

We cast forward emulation as an operator-learning task: given bathymetry and an initial free-surface disturbance, the model predicts a future trajectory generated by a stated numerical reference. Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) provide the primary controlled comparison \citep{li2021fno}. Factorized FNO, U-FNO, WNO, CNN, U-Net, and ConvLSTM configurations probe different global, local, multiscale, and recurrent inductive biases \citep{tran2023ffno,wen2022ufno,tripura2023wno,lecun1998gradient,ronneberger2015unet,shi2015convlstm}. These established architectures are instruments for testing the evaluation protocol; the contribution is not a new neural architecture.

Multi-reference evaluation additionally requires a shared temporal meaning. Equal tensor shapes do not imply equal physical states if frames are selected by solver-step index while adaptive step sizes differ. We therefore let every reference retain its natural internal steps but publish adjacent-step interpolants on one requested-time vector. This common-time protocol permits paired comparisons without forcing the solvers onto an identical internal step sequence.

Common-time alignment by itself is necessary good practice rather than a novel numerical method. The contribution evaluated here is the broader reference-aware protocol around that control: solver-specific natural stepping, shared requested output times, separate same-reference and cross-reference discrepancies, uncertainty estimates for the cross-reference ratios, numerical verification with prespecified criteria, and explicit interpretation limits.

Time alignment does not make one reference ground truth. Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR approximate the depth-averaged shallow-water equations with different discretizations, while the study-defined Boussinesq-type comparison introduces a linear weakly dispersive correction \citep{audusse2004hydrostatic,clain2016secondorder,shi2012boussinesq}. Their pairwise gaps combine equation-set and numerical effects. We therefore separate same-reference surrogate error, raw reference-to-reference discrepancy, and cross-reference surrogate discrepancy. A surrogate that lies closer to a second solver in one norm has not thereby become more physically accurate \citep{koehler2026emulator}.

The central question is consequently computational rather than operational: after the references are aligned to the same requested times and assessed under a fixed finite-horizon protocol, how strongly do the numerical reference and temporal-output protocol condition the apparent surrogate result? Distribution shift, resolution transfer, predictive uncertainty, and transfer to real bathymetric morphology are used as stress tests of that question, not as evidence of tsunami-warning readiness.

\noindent
The main contributions of this study are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=3.6em]
    \item a reference-aware evaluation protocol that combines identical scenarios, solver-specific natural stepping, 50 shared requested times, and separate same-reference, solver-gap, and cross-reference discrepancy measures,
    \item a numerical verification study combining shared-input identity, analytical and numerical tests, GeoClaw/Clawpack analytical, refinement, and representative inter-code comparisons, assessment of buffered-domain integration and requested-time extraction, and an independent 363-case sensitivity set,
    \item a quantitative comparison showing that the Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR discrepancy is $1.6$ and $2.1$ times the same-reference errors of the Hydrostatic FNO and F-FNO, respectively, while the shallow-water--Boussinesq gaps exceed the Boussinesq FNO error by more than five times,
    \item controlled demonstrations across eleven direct model--reference configurations, conditional windowed forecasts, distribution and resolution shifts, virtual-gauge diagnostics, a seven-member ensemble, and synthetic-forcing transfer to real bathymetric morphology.
\end{enumerate}
