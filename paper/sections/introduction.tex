\section{Introduction}
\setlength{\parskip}{1em}

Physics-based simulation of tsunami-like wave propagation can become expensive when repeated across many possible source scenarios, bathymetric fields, numerical resolutions, or uncertainty-analysis runs. This cost motivates surrogate models that approximate numerical rollouts more rapidly while retaining the spatial and temporal structure needed for scientific evaluation.

We cast forward emulation as an operator-learning problem: given a bathymetry field and an initial free-surface disturbance, the model predicts a future free-surface trajectory generated by a numerical reference solver. Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) are a natural primary model because they learn mappings between discretized functions and can be evaluated on spatial fields. We also include a factorized FNO (FFNO), mode-count ablations, U-FNO and WNO variants, while CNN and U-Net baselines provide simpler convolutional comparisons.

The goal is not to replace site-specific tsunami models or to claim operational early-warning readiness. Instead, we study a narrower scientific question: under controlled synthetic conditions, can neural surrogates provide useful speed--accuracy trade-offs for solver-generated tsunami-like wavefields, and how do those trade-offs change under distribution shift, resolution transfer, solver mismatch, predictive uncertainty, and transfer from synthetic to real bathymetry?

\noindent
The main contributions of this study are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=3.6em]
    \item A controlled forward benchmark that pairs procedurally generated bathymetry with diverse initial-displacement sources and shared numerical scenarios,
    \item a multi-reference label design with hydrostatic reconstruction as the primary reference, MUSCL-HR as a second-order comparison reference, and Boussinesq as a weakly dispersive comparison reference on the same scenarios,
    \item an FNO/FFNO-centered surrogate study with CNN, U-Net, U-FNO, WNO, and Fourier-mode ablations, together with seeded windowed FNO and FFNO variants for long-horizon rollout diagnostics,
    \item an evaluation suite covering solver validation, speed, metadata-filtered and strict held-out-family OOD diagnostics, cross-resolution behavior, solver-vs-solver analysis, emulator-superiority-style ratios, physics diagnostics, ensemble uncertainty, and a synthetic-source real-bathymetry morphology suite.
\end{enumerate}
