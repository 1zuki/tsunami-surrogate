\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a controlled common-time benchmark for neural emulation of solver-generated tsunami-like wavefields. Hydrostatic, MUSCL-HR, and Boussinesq publish the same 50 requested times while retaining their own natural time steps, on a central $64 \times 64$ crop of a buffered $96 \times 96$ computation. This makes the multi-reference comparisons about numerical-reference choice rather than mismatched label times.

Across the eleven selected single-seed direct model--reference configurations, F-FNO and ConvLSTM give the lowest Hydrostatic errors, while the base FNO remains close to U-FNO and the mode-count ablations. The same FNO behaves differently across references, and the common-time solver gaps show why: Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR are much closer to each other than either is to Boussinesq. Cross-reference ratios near one in several directions are benchmark-relative effects, not evidence that the surrogate is more accurate than its training solver. Seeded window models improve long-horizon error but use the first true target frame; strict family holdouts expose failures that metadata subgroups do not; and native-grid transfer remains limited.

The strongest transfer result is the fully wet real-bathymetry morphology suite, where direct F-FNO retains useful accuracy under synthetic forcing. The wet--dry stress failure and under-dispersed ensemble intervals show the boundary of that result. Inference is still hundreds to thousands of times faster than the matched vectorized CPU references, but this is an implementation comparison rather than an operational benchmark.

Future work needs surveyed bathymetry at physical scale, realistic rupture models, shoreline and wet--dry physics, stronger dispersive references, observed gauges, and comparisons with optimized production solvers. The present contribution is a controlled benchmark framework for asking how neural-surrogate conclusions depend on the numerical labels used to define them, not a finished tsunami-warning model.
