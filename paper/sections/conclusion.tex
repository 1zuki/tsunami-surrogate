\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study evaluates neural wavefield emulators against three numerical references on identical scenarios and shared requested times. Separating same-reference surrogate error, raw solver gaps, and cross-reference discrepancy shows that the reference can change the interpretation of model accuracy. With predictions held fixed, refining the Hydrostatic evaluation reference raises mean relative errors by $2.04\%$--$5.08\%$ and reverses some case-level model comparisons, while preserving the six-case aggregate ordering. Solver identity also reduces target-specific error in the pooled comparison, although all six tested cross-reference ratios remain above one.

The $92$-rollout full-horizon study finds contracting adjacent-grid differences, material boundary and damping sensitivity, roundoff-scale stationary drift, and nonzero moving-equilibrium drift. Matched refinement improves Hydrostatic agreement with GeoClaw in all three cases, while MUSCL-HR changes nonuniformly. These fixed-raster results strengthen numerical assessment without establishing formal convergence or physical truth.

Three-seed leading-model comparisons support bounded rank stability, while held-out-family, resolution-transfer, wet--dry, and uncertainty tests expose limits. In particular, near-nominal aggregate coverage does not establish difficult-subgroup reliability. Physical-scale bathymetry, realistic forcing, validated coastal physics, and observational comparisons remain necessary for operational use. The benchmark supports controlled forward-emulation research rather than event-scale tsunami forecasting.
