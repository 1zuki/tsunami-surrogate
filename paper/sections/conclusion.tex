\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work introduced a reference-aware evaluation protocol for neural wavefield emulators, isolating same-reference surrogate error, raw solver discrepancy, and cross-reference surrogate discrepancy across Hydrostatic, MUSCL-HR, and Boussinesq references. Aligning outputs to 50 shared requested output times on the central $64 \times 64$ crop of a buffered $96 \times 96$ computation prevents temporal sampling mismatch from being conflated with model error without assuming an absolute physical ground truth.

The reported numerical-reference gaps exceed selected same-reference emulator errors. Specifically, the common-time Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR RMSE gap is 1.6 and 2.1 times the Hydrostatic FNO and F-FNO errors, respectively, while the shallow-water--Boussinesq discrepancies exceed the same-reference Boussinesq FNO error by more than fivefold. For each of seeds 18, 36, and 67, F-FNO has the lowest checkpoint-level mean per-scenario Hydrostatic relative-$L_2$ within the replicated F-FNO, ConvLSTM, FNO, and U-Net subset. The near-unity cross-family ratios are benchmark-relative effects rather than evidence of physical generalization beyond the training references. Wet--dry stress failures and under-dispersed uncertainty bounds further mark the scope despite implementation-level speedups relative to the study's vectorized CPU references, not operational-solver or end-to-end latency measurements.

Future developments require physical-scale surveyed bathymetry, realistic fault rupture dynamics, wet--dry coastal boundary conditions, and direct assimilation of gauge observations. By establishing a controlled, transparent benchmarking framework, this study clarifies how conclusions regarding neural PDE surrogates depend directly on underlying numerical references and temporal alignment protocols.
