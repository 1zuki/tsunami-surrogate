\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We formulated and evaluated a reference-aware protocol for neural emulation of solver-generated tsunami-like wavefields. Hydrostatic, MUSCL-HR, and Boussinesq retain their own natural time steps but publish the same 50 requested times on a central $64 \times 64$ crop of a buffered $96 \times 96$ computation. Same-reference surrogate error, raw solver discrepancy, and cross-reference surrogate discrepancy are then reported separately rather than being conflated through mismatched output times or an assumed ground-truth solver.

The resulting reference gaps are not small relative to model error. The common-time Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR RMSE gap is $1.6$ times the Hydrostatic FNO error and $2.1$ times the F-FNO error, while the shallow-water--Boussinesq gaps exceed the same-reference Boussinesq FNO error by more than five times. Cross-reference ratios near one are therefore benchmark-relative effects, not evidence that a surrogate becomes physically more accurate than its training solver.

The eleven direct model--reference configurations, conditional windowed forecasts, strict holdouts, resolution studies, morphology transfer, and ensemble analysis act as stress tests of this protocol. F-FNO and ConvLSTM give the lowest selected single-seed Hydrostatic errors, but the architecture evidence is not a replicated population-level ranking. Fully wet real-bathymetry morphology transfer remains useful under synthetic forcing, whereas the wet--dry stress failure and under-dispersed ensemble intervals expose clear boundaries. Inference is hundreds to thousands of times faster than the matched vectorized CPU references, but this remains an implementation comparison rather than an operational benchmark.

Future work needs surveyed bathymetry at physical scale, realistic rupture models, shoreline and wet--dry physics, stronger dispersive references, observed gauges, and comparisons with optimized production solvers. The present contribution is a controlled computational framework for asking how neural PDE-surrogate conclusions depend on the numerical labels and temporal contract used to define them, not a finished tsunami-warning model or event-scale physical validation.
