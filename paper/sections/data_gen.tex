\section{Data-generation procedure}
\label{sec:data-generation}

\subsection{Benchmark design}
\label{subsec:benchmark-design}

The dataset is built around shared synthetic scenarios rather than solver-specific samples. Each scenario contains a procedurally generated bathymetry field, a synthetic initial-displacement source field, and metadata recording the sampled bathymetry family, source family, random strength factor, and file locations. Numerical reference trajectories are then produced by advancing the same scenario with one or more forward solvers. This separation between scenario generation and reference generation is important for multi-reference evaluation: when two solver labels differ, the difference is attributable to the numerical reference and not to a different random seabed or initial disturbance. Figure~\ref{fig:scenario-gallery} shows representative accepted scenario pairs spanning the morphology and source families described below.

The main benchmark contains three independently generated pools of $10{,}000$, $1{,}000$, and $2{,}500$ scenarios ($13{,}500$ in total). Separate split-qualified seeds and identities are used instead of randomly partitioning one pool, and the same authoritative scenario arrays are reused across the three numerical references. The numerical computation is performed on a $96 \times 96$ grid. The central $64 \times 64$ publication resolution is retained, while a 16-cell buffer and an 8-cell source taper separate the reported region from the outer boundary treatment.

Under the common-time protocol, target evaluation times are defined as: $t_k = 0.0035k, k = 1, \dots, 50$, terminating at $t = 0.175$ in normalized benchmark time. Each solver keeps its own natural steps: the shallow-water steps adapt to the evolving state, while the Boussinesq step is fixed within each scenario after a depth-based CFL calculation. Whenever a requested time is bracketed by two adjacent natural states, the generator interpolates only between those two states and emits the requested $\eta$ frame. This is output-only interpolation: it does not alter the solver update or force a shared internal time step. The initial state at $t = 0$ is stored for provenance but is not a supervised target.

The benchmark is intentionally synthetic and fully wet in its core configuration. Its purpose is to create a controlled supervised-learning problem for testing fast forward emulation of solver-generated tsunami-like wavefields, not to reproduce a specific coastline or provide operational hazard assessment. This design makes solver gaps, inference speed, distribution shift, resolution transfer, and uncertainty reproducible without presenting the synthetic fields as real events.

\subsection{Procedural bathymetry}
\label{subsec:procedural-bathymetry}

Bathymetry is represented by a signed bed-elevation field $b(x,y)$, where $b(x,y)\leq 0$ denotes submerged cells below mean sea level. In the default configuration, all generated fields are rescaled to the interval:
$$
[d_{\min}, d_{\max}] = [-10, -0.75].
$$
This formulation guarantees a wet domain throughout computation. The quiescent water depth $h_{\mathrm{rest}}(x,y)$ is evaluated as:
\begin{equation}
h_{\mathrm{rest}}(x,y)=\max\{-b(x,y)+\eta_{\mathrm{sea}},0\},
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{\mathrm{sea}}$ represents an adjustable sea-level offset parameter ($\eta_{\mathrm{sea}} = 0$ by default).

For each scenario, the bathymetry generator randomly selects a profile from five predefined terrain classes:
$$
\{\text{trench}, \text{continental}, \text{seamounts}, \text{canyon}, \text{island}\}.
$$

The generator first creates a low-frequency base field. In the current default configuration, this base is a weak rotated slope with slope components sampled from $[0.02,0.12]$, although flat and basin-like base modes are also supported. To mitigate spurious grid-aligned artifacts, a smooth spatial warping transformation is applied to the normalized coordinates prior to evaluating the bathymetric morphology:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
x_w=\operatorname{clip}(x+\alpha g_x(x,y),0,1), \\
y_w=\operatorname{clip}(y+\alpha g_y(x,y),0,1),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $g_x$ and $g_y$ denote Gaussian-filtered spatial displacement fields, and $\alpha$ specifies the warping intensity parameter.

The complete unnormalized bathymetric field $\tilde{b}(x,y)$ is synthesized via linear superposition of its constituent morphological components:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{b}(x,y) = b_{\mathrm{base}}(x,y) + b_{\mathrm{family}}(x_w,y_w) + \sum_i G_i(x,y) + \sum_j R_j(x,y) + N(x,y),
\end{equation}
where $b_{\mathrm{family}}$ is the dominant terrain-family term, $G_i$ are random Gaussian perturbations, $R_j$ are elongated ridge-like structures, and $N$ is multi-scale smoothed roughness. Trench and canyon samples emphasize elongated negative channels, seamount and island samples emphasize localized positive relief, and continental samples combine broad slopes with shelf-break-like structure. The stochastic Gaussian and ridge terms are sign-biased by family so that small-scale structure reinforces the selected morphology rather than erasing it.

To enforce the target depth bounds, the composite field $b(x,y)$ is mapped onto the interval $[d_{\min}, d_{\max}]$ via min–max normalization:
\begin{equation}
b(x,y) = d_{\min} + \frac{\tilde{b}(x,y) - \min_{x,y}\tilde{b}}{\max_{x,y}\tilde{b} - \min_{x,y}\tilde{b}} (d_{\max} - d_{\min}).
\end{equation}

This normalization deliberately prioritizes morphological diversity over site-specific amplitude realism. It also keeps the numerical problem bounded, which is useful when generating thousands of trajectories for model training. The procedural terrain construction follows the broader idea of synthetic terrain generation for controlled experiments, but the resulting fields should be interpreted as benchmark bathymetries rather than surveyed seafloor products \citep{musgrave1989terrain}.

\subsection{Initial-displacement sources}
\label{subsec:initial-displacement-sources}

The source generator produces a raw stored source template $q(x, y)$, saved as \texttt{source\_field}, on the same grid as the bathymetry. The default source family set is
$$
\{\texttt{gaussian},\ \texttt{multi-gauss},\ \texttt{dipole},\ \texttt{fault},\ \texttt{rough},\ \texttt{okada-like}\}.
$$
Gaussian and multi-Gaussian sources provide localized smooth uplift patterns. Dipole sources create paired uplift and subsidence lobes. Fault sources create elongated rotated uplift structures. Rough sources are generated from smoothed random fields and broaden the distribution of source geometries. The \texttt{okada-like} family samples simplified fault parameters such as length, width, slip, angle, dip, and source depth, and constructs an anisotropic deformation pattern inspired by elastic half-space source models \citep{okada1985surface}. It is used only as a synthetic source family, not as a full geophysical rupture model.

By default, the forward benchmark bypasses per-sample source normalization, thereby retaining the unscaled amplitude variation within each source class. The initial free-surface displacement field is then established by scaling the raw source template $q(x,y)$ with an independent strength factor $s \sim \mathcal{U}(0.45, 0.85)$:
\begin{equation}
\eta_0(x, y) = s\,q(x, y).
\end{equation}

The initial total fluid depth prescribed for the shallow-water solvers is defined as:
\begin{equation}
h_0(x,y) = \max\{h_{\mathrm{rest}}(x,y) + \eta_0(x,y), 0\},
\end{equation}
with the initial horizontal momentum fields set identically to zero. Consequently, each forward simulation originates from an initially quiescent, perturbed free surface over the synthetic bathymetry.

An auxiliary Boussinesq diagnostic configuration uses smoother bathymetries, lower source amplitudes, and a smaller source-strength range for propagation checks. The reported Boussinesq dataset uses the same bathymetry and source scenario definitions as the shallow-water solvers, so sample IDs stay aligned across targets.

\subsection{Reference rollouts and solver organization}
\label{subsec:reference-rollouts}

The primary reference labels are generated with finite-volume shallow-water solvers. The Hydrostatic solver is used as the main reference because it enforces nonnegative depth and is designed to preserve lake-at-rest balance over non-flat bathymetry \citep{audusse2004hydrostatic}. MUSCL-HR adds limited second-order reconstruction and a two-stage update, giving a less diffusive comparison reference on the same scenarios. The reported solver configurations use fixed target CFL numbers $0.1125$ and $0.225$ for Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR with state-adaptive time steps, radiation boundaries, and an elapsed-time-consistent cosine sponge. The Boussinesq comparison uses target CFL $0.35$, an open zero-gradient boundary, the same elapsed-time sponge convention, an optional smoothing filter that is disabled here, and a cached sparse-LU preconditioner for its conjugate-gradient acceleration solve.

For each shared scenario, every enabled reference produces a surface-elevation trajectory record: a compressed float32 \texttt{trajectory\_eta} is stored together with the requested times, adjacent-step brackets, interpolation weights, exact-knot flags, natural-step counts, quality summaries, and identifiers for the scenario and numerical configuration. Full internal states are not duplicated. For the shallow-water solvers, the free surface is reconstructed as follows:
\begin{equation}
\eta(x,y,t)=h(x,y,t)+b(x,y).
\end{equation}

For Boussinesq, $\eta$ is the evolved surface state. Scenario-level metadata record the shared scenario definition, while per-solver records link the corresponding labels to their numerical configurations. This layout allows single-solver training, paired cross-reference analysis, and reproduction of the requested-time extraction without resampling the bathymetry--source pair.

Numerical-admissibility criteria are applied during generation before a sample is accepted. The default policy rejects non-finite states, depth or positivity violations, excessive free-surface amplitude or velocity, sanitization, velocity clipping, projection, post-step CFL violations, and Boussinesq linear-solver failures. These criteria prevent unstable or numerically altered rollouts from entering the supervised-learning labels.

\subsection{Preprocessing and learning targets}
\label{subsec:preprocessing-targets}

Preprocessing converts accepted trajectory records into model-ready sharded arrays. The $10{,}000 / 1{,}000 / 2{,}500$ train/validation/test pools remain separate, and normalization is fit from the training pool only. Before analysis, each processed split is checked for the expected numerical references, array dimensions, metadata completeness, finite values, and traceability to the accepted raw trajectories.

The raw scenario stores the source template $q$, scalar \texttt{source\_strength} $s$, scaled perturbation \texttt{eta0}, initial depth $h_0$, and initial free surface separately. The default model inputs are bathymetry, the raw source template, and initial depth; the target is the 50-frame common-time $\eta$ trajectory. Optional initial-surface and solver-identity channels exist in the code but are not used by the direct single-reference models reported here.

Normalization statistics are computed from the training split only and reused unchanged for validation and test. Bathymetry and source channels and the trajectory target are standardized; initial depth remains in its stored scale. Processed archives retain sample identifiers, bathymetry/source families, solver name, source strength, and normalization parameters. The processed arrays were verified against the accepted common-time trajectory records before evaluation. The versioned data and evaluation release and the separate raw-trajectory mirror are identified in the data-availability statement; the repository alone should not be interpreted as the complete reproduction package.

\subsection{Distribution-shift and resolution-transfer datasets}
\label{subsec:shift-resolution-data}

The same metadata-first design supports controlled generalization tests. For source-family shift, bathymetry-family shift, and source-strength shift, the evaluation protocol builds filtered suites from the processed test data using the recorded source family, bathymetry family, and source strength. In the current configuration, these suites include the \texttt{multi-gauss} subset, the \texttt{trench} subset, and the high source-strength tail. These are difficult-subgroup diagnostics because the filtered families are still present in the training distribution.

We also build strict held-out-family datasets for four stress tests: \texttt{trench} and \texttt{continental} bathymetry holdouts, and \texttt{rough} and \texttt{okada-like} source holdouts. For these datasets the held-out family is removed before preprocessing and normalization statistics are refit from the filtered training split only. The held-out test subset is then evaluated separately from the in-distribution remainder of the same test pool, which keeps the scenario accounting explicit and avoids confusing metadata filtering with truly unseen-family generalization.

For cross-resolution analysis, we additionally generate paired native $32 \times 32$, $64 \times 64$, and $128 \times 128$ pools of $1{,}000$ scenarios per grid, split into $700/150/150$. One exact $128 \times 128$ master scenario is deterministically block-reduced to the lower grids before each solver rollout, and every resolution retains explicit common-time provenance. The reported native model matrix uses MUSCL-HR checkpoints trained separately at each grid and an explicit normalization bridge when a checkpoint is evaluated on another grid.

The real-bathymetry extension uses ten geographic crops from the GEBCO\_2026 Grid \citep{gebco2026}, selected to span trench, shelf--slope, island-arc, and basin morphologies. Each bounding-box crop is bilinearly resized to $64 \times 64$ and independently linearly rescaled into the fully wet training range $[-10, -0.75]$. This preserves within-crop relative morphology but not the original physical depth magnitude. The extension also contains one illustrative morphology crop, a fully wet coastline ablation, and a coastline wet--dry stress case. All use synthetic forcing and Hydrostatic labels. The stress suite alone permits dry-cell momentum zeroing and uses a suite-specific maximum-$|\eta|$ ceiling of $5.01$; positivity, finiteness, sanitization, velocity, and CFL criteria remain enforced. These data probe morphology and wet--dry distribution shift, not real-event forecasting.
