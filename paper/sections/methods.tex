\section{Problem formulation and evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:methods}

This section defines the supervised task, numerical references, validation boundaries, and evaluation metrics. Data construction is described in Section~\ref{sec:data-generation}; model and optimization details are given in Sections~\ref{sec:models} and~\ref{sec:training}.

\subsection{Forward emulation task}
\label{subsec:forward-task}
Let $\Omega = [0, 1]^2$ denote the normalized two-dimensional spatial domain. Each benchmark scenario is characterized by a bathymetric profile $b(x,y)$, a baseline source template $q(x,y)$, and an initial water depth $h_0(x,y)$. Under the convention where submerged regions satisfy $b(x,y) < 0$, the quiescent water depth field is defined as:
\begin{equation}
h_{\mathrm{rest}}(x, y) = \max\{-b(x, y) + \eta_{\mathrm{sea}}, 0\}.
\end{equation}
Given a sampled source-strength multiplier $s$, the initial free-surface perturbation $\eta_0(x,y)$ and total fluid thickness $h_0(x,y)$ are evaluated via:
\begin{equation}
\eta_0(x, y) = s\,q(x, y), \qquad h_0(x, y) = \max\{h_{\mathrm{rest}}(x, y) + \eta_0(x, y), 0\}.
\end{equation}
For the supervised surrogate modeling task, the default input representation concatenates these spatial fields into a three-channel tensor:
\begin{equation}
X = [\,b, q, h_0\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times H \times W}.
\end{equation}

Note that the input tensor incorporates the raw source profile $q$ instead of the scaled perturbation $\eta_0$; both quantities remain archived in the underlying data provenance. For a given numerical solver reference $r$, the target trajectory $Y_r$ consists of 50 discrete temporal snapshots defined on a $64 \times 64$ spatial grid:
\begin{equation}
Y_r = \left[\eta_r(t_1), \dots, \eta_r(t_{50})\right] \in \mathbb{R}^{50 \times 64 \times 64},
\end{equation}
sampled at the requested evaluation times $t_k = 0.0035k$ ($k = 1, \dots, 50$).

The final target snapshot is evaluated at $t = 0.175$ in normalized time, whereas the initial state ($t = 0$) is omitted from the target sequence. The underlying coordinate system governs an abstract benchmark domain and carries no implicit rescaling to dimensional physical time or specific oceanic bathymetry scales.

\subsection{Common-time extraction and buffered domain}
\label{subsec:common-time-extraction}

Each reference advances on its own natural time sequence $\tau_r ^ 0 < \tau_r ^ 1 < \cdots$. The shallow-water steps adapt to the evolving state, whereas the Boussinesq step is CFL-selected once from the fixed depth field of each scenario and remains constant within that rollout.
When a target time $t_k$ falls between two consecutive solver steps ($\tau_r^n < t_k < \tau_r^{n+1}$), the interpolation weight $\lambda_{r,k} \in (0, 1)$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{r, k} = \frac{t_k - \tau_r ^ n}{\tau_r ^ {n + 1} - \tau_r ^ n}
\end{equation}
The corresponding output state is then linearly reconstructed as:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{Z}_r(t_k) = (1 - \lambda_{r, k}) Z_r^n + \lambda_{r, k} Z_r^{n+1}
\end{equation}

For the shallow-water references, $Z = [h, hu, hv] ^ \top$ and the free surface is then recovered using the time-independent bathymetry; for Boussinesq, $\eta$ is already part of the evolved state. If $t_k$ exactly equals a natural time, the corresponding state is copied without interpolation. Several requested times may fall inside one natural-step interval, but every output is formed only from that adjacent pair.

The interpolation is performed in float64 before the reported frame is cast to float32. It is output-only: interpolated states are never fed back into the time integrator, so the natural solver trajectory is unchanged. For each reported frame, the generator records the bracketing times, interpolation weight, bracket width, exact-knot status, and natural-step index. This design prevents the cross-reference time mismatch that would arise from solver-step-indexed sampling while retaining solver-specific natural stepping. It does not remove the interpolation approximation or make the three governing models equivalent.

The central publication domain contains $64 \times 64$ cells with $\Delta x = \Delta y = 1/64$. Numerical integration is performed on $96 \times 96$ cells at the same spacing, adding 16 cells around every side rather than refining the reported region. Bathymetry is extended by its edge values into the buffer, and the source is tapered over the outer eight buffer cells. Only the central crop enters the learning target. The buffer and elapsed-time sponge reduce direct boundary exposure over the finite horizon, but they do not establish a generally non-reflecting boundary condition.

The surrogate $f_\theta$ approximates the solver-conditioned mapping operator:
\begin{equation}
f_\theta: X \mapsto \widehat{Y}_r \approx Y_r.
\end{equation}
In this setup, the hydrostatic reconstruction scheme serves as the primary numerical reference. A second shallow-water reference is provided by a MUSCL-HR solver, while a weakly dispersive Boussinesq model is included as an exploratory comparison reference.

\subsection{Numerical references}
\label{subsec:numerical-reference-formulation}
The reference shallow-water dynamics govern the state vector $U = [\, h, \, hu, \, hv \,]^\top$ (with free-surface elevation $\eta = h + b$) via the conservation law:
\begin{equation}
\partial_t U + \partial_x F(U) + \partial_y G(U) = S(U, b),
\end{equation}
with flux functions $F(U), G(U)$ and bed-slope source $S(U, b)$ specified as:
\begin{equation}
F(U) =
\begin{bmatrix}
    hu \\[2pt]
    \dfrac{(hu) ^ 2}{h} + \dfrac{1}{2}gh ^ 2 \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{hu\, hv}{h}    
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
G(U) =
\begin{bmatrix}
    hv \\[2pt]
    \dfrac{hu\, hv}{h} \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{(hv) ^ 2}{h} + \dfrac{1}{2}gh ^ 2
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
S(U, b) =
\begin{bmatrix}
    0 \\
    -gh\, \partial_x b \\
    -gh\, \partial_y b
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Here, $h$ is the fluid depth and $(hu, hv)$ are the momentum components, defining velocities $(u, v) = (hu/h, hv/h)$ in wet cells. To prevent division by zero, depths below a dry tolerance are regularized and assigned zero momentum. Although the domain remains fully wet by design, these safeguards ensure that any loss of positivity triggers an explicit numerical error rather than undefined states.

All spatial coordinates, depths, elevations, velocities, and times in these equations are benchmark quantities rather than SI measurements for a particular ocean basin. The resolved numerical coefficients are $g = 9.81$, dry tolerance $10^{-6}$, and maximum safeguarded velocity $30$ in those benchmark units. The familiar numerical value of $g$ does not by itself define a dimensional length--time mapping.

Both shallow-water baselines adopt finite-volume updates utilizing Rusanov (Lax–Friedrichs) intercell fluxes:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{F}(U_L, U_R) = \frac{1}{2}\left(F(U_L) + F(U_R)\right) - \frac{1}{2} \alpha_x(U_R - U_L),
\\
\alpha_x = \max\!\left\{|u_L| + \sqrt{gh_L},\, |u_R| + \sqrt{gh_R}\right\},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
and similarly for $\mathcal{G}$ in the $y$-direction \citep{toro2009riemann,toro2026rusanov}. The numerical conservative update reads:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
U_{i,j}^{n + 1} = U_{i, j}^{n}
&- \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}
\left(\mathcal{F}_{i + 1/2, j} - \mathcal{F}_{i - 1/2, j}\right) \\
&- \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y}
\left(\mathcal{G}_{i, j + 1/2} - \mathcal{G}_{i, j - 1/2}\right)
+ \Delta t\,S_{i, j}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The well-balanced source treatment is incorporated differently by the two schemes. The Rusanov dissipation uses the largest local gravity-wave characteristic speed crossing each face. It is deliberately simple and robust: the principal difference between the two shallow-water references comes from their reconstruction and time integration, not from changing the approximate Riemann solver.

To satisfy the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) condition, the adaptive time step is evaluated as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta t = c_r\left[\max\!\left\{\frac{\max(|u| + \sqrt{gh})}{\Delta x}, \frac{\max(|v| + \sqrt{gh})}{\Delta y} \right\} \right] ^ {-1},
\end{equation}
where the Courant number $c_r$ is prescribed as $c_r = 0.1125$ for the Hydrostatic solver and $c_r = 0.225$ for the MUSCL-HR scheme. The wave speeds and therefore $\Delta t$ are recomputed from every natural state. The final step crossing a requested output time is not shortened to land on that time; instead, the two natural states continue to bracket the request and the output-only interpolation of Section~\ref{subsec:common-time-extraction} is applied.

\paragraph{Hydrostatic reconstruction reference.} The primary shallow-water baseline employs cell-centered states without slope reconstruction, advancing solutions via a first-order explicit finite-volume update. Interface water depths are reconstructed following Audusse et al. \citep{audusse2004hydrostatic}:
\begin{equation}
z ^ \star = \max\{b_L, b_R\}, \qquad
h_L ^ \star = \max\{0, h_L + b_L - z ^ \star\}, \qquad
h_R ^ \star = \max\{0, h_R + b_R - z ^ \star\},
\end{equation}
ensuring positivity preservation and the exact lake-at-rest condition (C-property). Reconstructed momenta retain host-cell velocities, with interface-specific pressure corrections absorbing local bathymetric gradients. By directly coupling topographic sources with interface flux differences, this formulation avoids ad-hoc bed-slope approximations. As a well-balanced, first-order scheme, it provides a robust baseline for evaluating higher-order models and neural surrogates.

\paragraph{MUSCL-HR reference.}
The MUSCL-HR scheme independently reconstructs $h$, $\eta = h + b$, $u$, and $v$ along each spatial dimension. For a scalar cell-centered field $q$, interface values are reconstructed using the componentwise minmod limiter:
\begin{equation}
s_i = \operatorname{minmod}(q_i - q_{i - 1},\, q_{i + 1} - q_i),
\qquad
q_i ^ \pm = q_i \pm\frac{1}{2}s_i.
\end{equation}
which zeroes gradients at local extrema to enforce TVD stability, reverting locally to first-order accuracy near discontinuities. Reconstructed depths are constrained to be non-negative, face velocities are clamped to the prescribed maximum speed bound, and face bathymetry is consistently retrieved via $b ^ \pm = \eta ^ \pm - h ^ \pm$. Hydrostatic reconstruction and the Rusanov flux are subsequently applied to these face states \citep{clain2016secondorder,hou2015muscl}.

\paragraph{Topographic source discretization}. To preserve well-balancedness, each Euler stage incorporates the reconstructed bed-slope momentum source:
\begin{equation}
S_{hu} = -\frac{g}{2}(h_E + h_W)\frac{b_E - b_W}{\Delta x},
\qquad
S_{hv} = -\frac{g}{2}(h_N + h_S)\frac{b_N - b_S}{\Delta y},
\end{equation}
where subscripts $\{E, W, N, S\}$ denote limited face states reconstructed at cell boundaries. Applied prior to the Heun stage averaging, this source term operates alongside hydrostatic interface corrections to support topographic balance.

Time integration uses a two-stage Heun form. Denoting one explicit limited finite-volume Euler step by $\Phi_{\Delta t}(U)$, the cell update reads:
\begin{equation}
U ^ {(1)} = \Phi_{\Delta t}(U ^ n),\qquad
U ^ {n + 1} = \frac{1}{2} U ^ n +
\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{\Delta t}\!\left(U ^ {(1)}\right).
\end{equation}

Positivity and dry-momentum projections are checked after the stage updates and after the final average. Consequently, MUSCL-HR differs from the primary reference through both its piecewise-linear spatial reconstruction and two-stage temporal integration, rather than a simple CFL adjustment.

\paragraph{Weakly dispersive Boussinesq comparison.}
The Boussinesq comparison evolves surface elevation and its time derivative, $[\eta, \eta_t] ^ \top$, rather than depth and discharge. We define it as a study-specific linear, elevation-only dispersive benchmark rather than an implementation of a complete coastal Boussinesq model. Its variable-depth elliptic acceleration system is:
\begin{equation}
\left(I - \alpha_B \nabla \cdot(H ^ 2 \nabla)\right) a_\eta = g \nabla \cdot(H \nabla \eta),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
H(x, y) = \max\!\left\{(-b(x, y) + \eta_{\mathrm{sea}}) s_H, H_{\min}\right\}.
\end{equation}
We use $\alpha_B = 1/3$, $s_H = 1$, and $H_{\min} = 10 ^ {-4}$. Spatial gradients and variable-coefficient divergences are represented by centered second-order finite differences with depth coefficients averaged to cell faces. For fixed bathymetry, the dispersive mass operator on the left-hand side is unchanged throughout a rollout. Its face coefficients and sparse factorization are therefore cached, and each matrix-free conjugate-gradient acceleration solve uses the cached sparse-LU factor as a preconditioner. The relative residual tolerance is $10 ^ {-10}$ with at most $750$ iterations per solve.

Two acceleration solves form one velocity-Verlet step:
\begin{equation}
\eta ^ {n + 1} = \eta ^ n + \Delta t\, \eta_t ^ n + \frac{1}{2}\Delta t ^ 2a_\eta ^ n,
\qquad
\eta_t ^ {n + 1} = \eta_t ^ n + \frac{\Delta t}{2} \left(a_\eta ^ n + a_\eta ^ {n + 1} \right).
\end{equation}
Samples failing residual tolerance in either acceleration solve are discarded. For a fixed bathymetry, the time step is determined by the maximum long-wave speed:
\begin{equation}
\Delta t = \frac{0.35}{\sqrt{g\max(H)}\left(\Delta x ^ {-1} + \Delta y ^ {-1}\right)}.
\end{equation}
Designed as a linear, elevation-only benchmark, this weakly dispersive formulation provides a controlled equation-family contrast without serving as a full nonlinear coastal Boussinesq model or implying superior physical accuracy over the shallow-water references.

\paragraph{Boundaries, damping, and shared scope.}
The shallow-water references use bathymetry-aware linearized radiation states at all four outer edges. The outgoing free-surface characteristic is continued through the face around the local rest depth, while the tangential velocity is carried from the adjacent interior cell. This treatment reduces direct reflection for small outgoing waves but is not an exact non-reflecting condition for nonlinear, oblique, or strongly heterogeneous propagation. The Boussinesq comparison instead uses open zero-gradient scalar boundaries.

All references apply a 16-cell cosine sponge layer within the outer domain boundary. Given reference mask $D(x, y) \in [0.8, 1]$ and baseline step $\Delta t_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0.0035$, per-step damping follows $D^{\Delta t/\Delta t_{\mathrm{ref}}}$, ensuring uniform cumulative dissipation over equivalent elapsed benchmark times regardless of step size. The shallow-water sponge damps momentum and free-surface displacement about rest depth; the Boussinesq sponge damps $\eta$ and $\eta_t$. The optional Boussinesq smoothing filter is disabled. Across all three references, the benchmark omits Coriolis acceleration, bottom friction, moving bathymetry, and event-specific forcing after initialization.

\subsection{Numerical verification and validation scope}
\label{subsec:reference-validation}

The numerical-reference and temporal-output protocol was assessed before the final surrogate analysis through five complementary forms of evidence:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
    \item \textbf{Input identity:} all $13{,}500$ scenario definitions were checked for split-qualified identity, array equality, horizon coverage, and exact reuse across the three references.
    \item \textbf{Internal verification:} analytical-wave, lake-at-rest, conservation, spatial/temporal sensitivity, requested-output, boundary/sponge, dispersive, numerical-stability, and deterministic-repeatability checks were run with thresholds fixed before evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Independent comparison:} matched analytical and representative finite-horizon cases were compared with GeoClaw/Clawpack 5.14.0 under the archived configuration \citep{leveque2011tsunami,berger2011geoclaw}. The analytical and refinement comparisons satisfied their criteria, but most exploratory full-configuration comparisons exceeded their descriptive limits. The external solver is therefore an independent comparator, not automatic truth or evidence of pointwise equivalence.
    \item \textbf{Integrated numerical procedure:} the buffered-domain integration, adaptive stepping, requested-time interpolation, and quality screening were assessed on cases separate from the lower-level implementation checks.
    \item \textbf{Independent sensitivity set:} additional training-pool cases, excluded from the earlier diagnostic selections, tested the reported and comparison CFL settings, time extraction, numerical-admissibility criteria, and repeatability.
\end{enumerate}

For three prespecified representative finite-horizon shallow-water cases, a controlled follow-up ablation removes the sponge, changes the outer boundary from radiation to open, moves that boundary farther from the reported crop at unchanged cell spacing, and then halves the in-house cell size on the original physical domain. The comparison is descriptive: it identifies sensitivity among the tested configurations but is not a formal convergence study and does not isolate one numerical component.

Decision thresholds and case selections were fixed before the corresponding outcomes were inspected. Validation and test scenarios used to evaluate the surrogates were not used to tune these numerical acceptance criteria. The complete verification program was repeated without altering the numerical implementation before the reported model evaluation. Satisfying these criteria supports the implemented finite-horizon protocol; it does not establish observed-event validity, shoreline accuracy, generally non-reflecting boundaries, or equivalence with an operational tsunami code.

Each dataset trajectory is additionally screened for finiteness, positivity/depth, amplitude, velocity, sanitization, projection, clipping, post-step CFL, and, where relevant, conjugate-gradient convergence. Trajectories that do not satisfy these criteria are excluded.

\subsection{Field and physics metrics}
\label{subsec:eval-metrics}
Field errors are evaluated elementwise across entire trajectories using standard metrics:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MAE} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_k | \widehat{y}_k - y_k |,
\qquad
\mathrm{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_k(\widehat{y}_k - y_k) ^ 2},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{rel}\, L_2 = \frac{\|\widehat{y} - y \|_2}{\| y \|_2},
\qquad
\mathrm{max\text{-}err}=\max_k | \widehat{y}_k - y_k |.
\end{equation}
where $k$ indexes all space-time grid points. MAE, RMSE, and relative $L_2$ are computed in denormalized benchmark-scale units. Maximum absolute error ($\mathrm{max\text{-}err}$) is reported in normalized target units unless explicitly noted otherwise.

For a full split, the underlying sums are accumulated before reduction, giving one global relative $L_2$ and one global maximum. Metadata subgroup tables instead compute per-scenario metrics first and then average within the group. These two aggregations answer different questions and are labelled separately.

For the selected single-seed Hydrostatic architecture comparison, we also use a paired nonparametric bootstrap: one shared index draw resamples the same $2{,}500$ scenario identities $2{,}000$ times with seed $20260814$. We report mean per-scenario denormalized relative $L_2$ and paired differences from FNO. These are test-scenario intervals for fixed checkpoints, not training-seed uncertainty or population-level architecture rankings.

Training-seed variation is assessed separately with seeds $\{18, 36, 67\}$ for Hydrostatic FNO, F-FNO, ConvLSTM, and U-Net, together with FNO trained on MUSCL-HR and Boussinesq. Every checkpoint is evaluated on the same held-out scenarios. We report the mean, sample standard deviation, and range of the three checkpoint-level mean per-scenario metrics. With only three seeds, these summaries are descriptive and are not treated as confidence intervals. Architectures outside this replicated subset retain their selected single-seed results.

Per-frame relative $L_2$ errors evaluate spatial slices at specific time instances. Physical conservation properties are monitored via domain-averaged quantities:
\begin{equation}
\overline{\eta}(t) = \frac{1}{HW}\sum_{i, j}\eta_{i, j}(t),
\qquad
\overline{h}(t) = \frac{1}{HW}\sum_{i, j}\bigl(\eta_{i, j}(t) - b_{i, j}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\overline{h}$ is a fully wet mass proxy rather than an independently conserved model output. Spectral error is computed from orthonormal two-dimensional FFT coefficients in radial wavenumber bands $\kappa \leq 8$, $8 < \kappa \leq 16$, and $\kappa > 16$:
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathcal{B}} = \frac{\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{F}(\widehat{Y} - Y)\|_2}{\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{F}(Y)\|_2}.
\end{equation}
These diagnostics localize error in integral and spectral structure; none of the neural models enforces a conservation law.

\subsection{Generalization and resolution transfer}
\label{subsec:generalization-protocol}

Metadata-filtered suites select \texttt{multi-gauss}, \texttt{trench}, or source strength $s \ge 0.82$ from the ordinary test set. These are difficult subgroups within the training support, not unseen-family OOD datasets. The strict tests remove one bathymetry or source family before preprocessing and training, refit normalization on the filtered training set, and evaluate the excluded family separately. When a checkpoint is applied to data standardized with another training distribution, the inputs are first restored with the dataset statistics and then re-standardized with the checkpoint's training statistics. Predictions and targets are finally compared after target denormalization in one benchmark-scale space.

The proxy resolution diagnostic resizes the processed Hydrostatic test fields to $32 \times 32$ or $128 \times 128$ and applies the same $64 \times 64$ checkpoint. The native experiment instead uses paired MUSCL-HR simulations at $32$, $64$, and $128$ resolution. Three checkpoints are trained separately, and the full train-grid/test-grid matrix is evaluated. Each target dataset is rebased into the checkpoint's training normalization before inference.

\subsection{Runtime benchmarking}
\label{subsec:runtime}

The numerical references are timed on CPU using the common-time $96 \times 96$ rollout and central-crop protocol, excluding bathymetry and source generation. These are vectorized NumPy/SciPy implementations. Each reference timing uses eight fixed scenarios, one warm-up pass, and three measured repeats. Surrogates are timed on the RTX 4050 Laptop GPU in fp32 at batch size one, with TF32 disabled, five warm-up passes, twenty measured passes, and device synchronization around timing. Implementation speedup is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{speedup} = \frac{T_{\mathrm{reference}}}{T_{\mathrm{surrogate}}},
\end{equation}
where $T$ denotes average rollout time per sample.

Loading the stored scenario, preparing the buffered inputs, and constructing the solver are recorded separately from the rollout denominator. The comparison therefore measures the repeated forward task under stated hardware, not end-to-end hazard-system latency. The denominator is not a compiled, GPU, adaptive-mesh, or operational tsunami solver.

\subsection{Cross-reference discrepancy}
\label{subsec:cross-solver}

The raw solver gap compares two numerical references on the same scenarios and requested times. A second diagnostic compares a surrogate trained on reference $A$ with reference $B$:
\begin{equation}
\rho(A \rightarrow B) = 
\frac{\mathrm{RMSE}(f_\theta ^ A, \mathrm{ref}\, B)}
     {\mathrm{RMSE}(\mathrm{ref}\, A, \mathrm{ref}\, B)}.
\end{equation}
All terms use the global denormalized benchmark-scale RMSE, and confidence intervals use 2,000 scenario bootstrap resamples. The ratio is also evaluated independently for the three FNO training seeds $\{18, 36, 67\}$; their mean, sample standard deviation, and range are reported separately from the within-checkpoint scenario-bootstrap interval. A value $\rho < 1$ means that the surrogate is closer to reference $B$ in this benchmark metric than reference $A$ is. Without observational truth or a stronger physical reference, this is not evidence of physical superiority.

\subsection{Gauge, arrival, and peak diagnostics}
\label{subsec:wave-metrics}

We evaluate nine fixed virtual gauges arranged on a $3 \times 3$ interior grid. For target waveform $y_g(t_k)$ and prediction $\widehat{y}_g(t_k)$, the waveform normalized RMSE is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NRMSE}_g = \frac{\sqrt{T ^ {-1} \sum_k \bigl(\widehat{y}_g(t_k) - y_g(t_k) \bigr) ^ 2}}{\max_k | y_g(t_k) |}.
\end{equation}

Gauges with target amplitude at or below $10 ^ {-12}$ are excluded from waveform NRMSE and relative-peak summaries and counted as inactive, avoiding division by a numerically zero target. The gauge-arrival threshold is $10\%$ of the target gauge's maximum absolute amplitude and is never changed by the prediction. A target gauge that never crosses the threshold is inactive; one already active at $t_1$ is excluded because the true arrival occurred before the observed window. An eligible gauge is compared only if the prediction also crosses the threshold, and missing predicted arrivals are counted separately. Peak elevation is the maximum signed surface value. To reduce one-frame instability in the time-to-peak metric, all times within $1\%$ of the target or predicted peak-to-trough range are treated as a plateau and averaged.

A separate arrival-map diagnostic uses a single target-only threshold equal to $10\%$ of the sample-wide maximum absolute target elevation, applies it at every pixel, and reports requested-frame index differences only where both prediction and target cross. Its eligibility fraction is reported together with the error. The summary first computes mean absolute index error over the eligible pixels of each scenario and then averages those scenario values; it does not pool all eligible pixels into one global reduction. These are normalized-domain virtual-observation diagnostics, not comparisons with measured coastal gauges.

\subsection{Uncertainty quantification}
\label{subsec:uncertainty}
A seven-member FNO deep ensemble differs only in seed \citep{lakshminarayanan2017deepensembles}. For member predictions $\widehat{Y} ^ {(m)}$, the ensemble mean $\bar{Y}$ and variance $\sigma^2$ are computed pointwise via:
\begin{equation}
\bar{Y} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m = 1} ^ {M}\widehat{Y} ^ {(m)},
\qquad
\sigma ^ 2 = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m = 1} ^ {M}\left(\widehat{Y} ^ {(m)} - \bar{Y}\right) ^ 2.
\end{equation}
Under a Gaussian error assumption, the central prediction interval corresponding to a nominal coverage probability $p$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
\bar{Y}\pm z_p\sigma,
\qquad
z_p = \sqrt{2}\, \operatorname{erf} ^ {-1}(p).
\end{equation}
We report the empirical coverage and the Pearson correlation coefficient between $\sigma$ and $|\bar{Y} - Y|$. As a study-defined interval-scale correction for raw ensemble under-dispersion, a single scalar variance scaling factor $\gamma$ is estimated using only the validation subset:
\begin{equation}
\gamma = \max\left\{1,\, \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{val}}} \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{val}}} \frac{(Y_k - \bar{Y}_k)^2}{\max(\sigma_k^2, \varepsilon)}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The calibrated predictive interval is obtained by rescaling $\sigma$ to $\sqrt{\gamma}\sigma$, applying this factor directly to both the test and subgroup evaluation sets without further tuning. Because the empirical coverage is computed marginally across correlated pixels and frames, these metrics serve as interval-scale reliability diagnostics rather than calibrated event probabilities.
