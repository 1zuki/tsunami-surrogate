\section{Problem formulation and evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:methods}

This section defines the supervised task, numerical references, validation boundaries, and evaluation metrics. Data construction is described in Section~\ref{sec:data-generation}; model and optimization details are given in Sections~\ref{sec:models} and~\ref{sec:training}.

\subsection{Forward emulation task}
\label{subsec:forward-task}

Let $\Omega=[0,1]^2$ be the normalized two-dimensional publication domain. Each scenario contains bathymetry $b$, a raw source template $q$, and initial water depth $h_0$. The convention is $b<0$ for submerged cells, giving
\[
h_{\mathrm{rest}}(x, y) = \max\{-b(x, y) + \eta_{\mathrm{sea}}, 0\}.
\]
With sampled source-strength multiplier $s$,
\[
\eta_0(x, y) = s\, q(x, y), \qquad
h_0(x, y) = \max\{h_{\mathrm{rest}}(x, y) + \eta_0(x, y), 0\}.
\]
The default supervised input is
\[
X = [\, b, q, h_0\,] \in\mathbb{R} ^ {3 \times H \times W}.
\]
The source channel is the raw template $q$ rather than the scaled perturbation $\eta_0$; both quantities remain available in the raw provenance.

For numerical reference $r$, the target is
\[
Y_r = [\,\eta_r(t_1), \ldots, \eta_r(t_{50})\,]
\in\mathbb{R} ^ {50 \times 64 \times64},
\]
where
\[
t_k = 0.0035k, \qquad k = 1, \ldots1, 50.
\]
The final requested time is $0.175$ in normalized benchmark-time units. The initial state at $t=0$ is not a target. These coordinates and times define a benchmark-scale problem; no conversion to a particular ocean-basin length or dimensional event duration is implied.

\subsection{Common-time extraction and buffered domain}
\label{subsec:common-time-extraction}

Each reference advances on its own natural time sequence $\tau_r ^ 0 < \tau_r ^ 1 < \cdot s$. The shallow-water steps adapt to the evolving state, whereas the Boussinesq step is CFL-selected once from the fixed depth field of each scenario and remains constant within that rollout. For a requested time $t_k$ bracketed by two adjacent natural states, $\tau_r ^ n < t_k < \tau_r ^ {n + 1}$, the extraction weight is
\[
\lambda_{r, k} = \frac{t_k - \tau_r ^ n}{\tau_r ^ {n + 1} - \tau_r ^ n},
\qquad 0 < \lambda_{r, k} < 1,
\]
and the published state is
\[
\widetilde{Z}_r (t_k) = (1 - \lambda_{r, k}) Z_r ^ n + \lambda_{r, k} Z_r ^ {n + 1}.
\]
For the shallow-water references, $Z = [h, hu, hv] ^ \top$ and the free surface is then recovered using the time-independent bathymetry; for Boussinesq, $\eta$ is already part of the evolved state. If $t_k$ exactly equals a natural time, the corresponding state is copied without interpolation. Several requested times may fall inside one natural-step interval, but every output is formed only from that adjacent pair.

The interpolation is performed in float64 before the publication frame is case to float32. It is output-only: interpolated states are never fed back into the time integrator, so the natural solver trajectory is unchanged. For each published frame, the generator records the bracketing times, interpolation weight, bracked width, exact-knot status, and natural-step index. This design prevents the cross-reference time mismatch that would arise from solver-step-indexed sampling while retaining solver-specific natural stepping, It does not remove the interpolation approximation or make the three governing models equivalent.

The central publication domain contains $64 \times 64$ cells with $\Delta x = \Delta y = 1/64$. Numerical integration is performed on $96 \times 96$ cells at the same spacing, adding 16 cells around every side rather than refining the reported region. Bathymetry is extended by its edge values into the buffer, and the source is tapered over the outer eight buffer cells. Only the central crop enters the learning target. The buffer and elapsed-time sponge reduce direct boundary exposure over the finite horizon, but they do not establish a generally non-reflecting boundary condition.

A surrogate $f_\theta$ approximates the solver-conditioned operator
\[
f_\theta: X \mapsto \widehat{Y}_r \approx Y_r.
\]
Hydrostatic reconstruction is the primary reference. MUSCL-HR is a second shallow-water reference, and Boussinesq is an exploratory weakly dispersive comparison reference.

\subsection{Numerical references}
\label{subsec:numerical-reference-formulation}

The shallow-water references evolve
\[
U = [\, h, hu, hv \,] ^ \top, \qquad \eta = h + b,
\]
under
\[
\partial_t U + \partial_x F(U) + \partial_y G(U) = S(U, b),
\]
with
\[
F(U) =
\begin{bmatrix}
    hu \\[2pt]
    \dfrac{(hu) ^ 2}{h} + \dfrac{1}{2}gh ^ 2 \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{hu\, hv}{h}    
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
G(U) =
\begin{bmatrix}
    hv \\[2pt]
    \dfrac{hu\, hv}{h} \\[6pt]
    \dfrac{(hv) ^ 2}{h} + \dfrac{1}{2}gh ^ 2
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
S(U, b) =
\begin{bmatrix}
    0 \\
    -gh\, \partial_x b \\
    -gh\, \partial_y b
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Here $h$ is water depth, $hu$ and $hv$ are the two depth-integrated discharges, and $u = hu/h$, $v = hv/h$ wherever the cell is wet. The numerical unknowns are cell averages. Divisions use a minimum positive depth, and cells at or below the configured dry tolerance have zero momentum. Although the common-time scenarios are designed to remain fully wet, retaining these safeguards makes loss of positivity a detectable numerical failure rather than an undefined velocity.

All spatial coordinates, depths, elevations, velocities, and times in these equations are benchmark quantities rather than SI measurements for a particular ocean basin. The resolved numerical coefficients are $g = 9.81$, dry tolerance $10 ^ {-6}$, and maximum safeguarded velocity $30$ in those benchmark units. The choice of the familiar numerical value for $g$ does not by itself define a dimensional length--time mapping.

Both references use finite-volume updates with Rusanov-type interface fluxes,
\[
\mathcal{F}(U_L, U_R) = \frac{1}{2}\left(F(U_L) + F(U_R)\right) - \frac{1}{2} \alpha_x(U_R - U_L),
\]
where
\[
\alpha_x = \max\!\left\{|u_L| + \sqrt{gh_L},\, |u_R| + \sqrt{gh_R}\right\},
\]
and similarly in $y$ \citep{toro2009riemann,toro2026rusanov}. Schematically, the cell update is
\[
U_{i,j} ^ {n + 1} = U_{i, j} ^ {n} - \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}
\left(\mathcal{F}_{i + 1/2, j} - \mathcal{F}_{i - 1/2, j}\right)
- \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y}
\left(\mathcal{G}_{i, j + 1/2} - \mathcal{G}_{i, j - 1/2}\right)
+ \Delta t\,S_{i, j},
\]
with the well-balanced source treatment incorporated differently by the two schemes. The Rusanov dissipation uses the largest local gravity-wave characteristic speed crossing each face. It is deliberately simple and robust: the principal difference between the two shallow-water references comes from their reconstruction and time integration, not from changing the approximate Riemann solver.

The shallow-water adaptive step is
\[
\Delta t = c_r\left[\max\!\left\{\frac{\max(|u| + \sqrt{gh})}{\Delta x}, \frac{\max(|v| + \sqrt{gh})}{\Delta y} \right\} \right] ^ {-1},
\]
where $c_r = 0.1125$ for Hydrostatic and $c_r = 0.225$ for MUSCL-HR. The wave speeds and therefore $\Delta t$ are recomputed from every natural state. The final step crossing a requested output time is not shortened to land on that time; instead, the two natural states continue to bracket the request and the output-only interpolation of Section~\ref{subsec:common-time-extraction} is applied.

\paragraph{Hydrostatic reconstruction reference.}
The primary reference uses cell-centered states without piecewise-linear slope reconstruction and advances them with one explicit finite-volume update per natural step. At a face, hydrostatic reconstruction replaces the two depths by
\[
z ^ \star = \max\{b_L, b_R\}, \qquad
h_L ^ \star = \max\{0, h_L + b_L - z ^ \star\}, \qquad
h_R ^ \star = \max\{0, h_R + b_R - z ^ \star\},
\]
which preserves nonnegative depth and lake-at-rest balance \citep{audusse2004hydrostatic}. The reconstructed momenta retain the velocities of their originating cells, while side-specific pressure corrections account for the portion of the hydrostatic pressure removed by the depth reconstruction. This couples the topographic source and flux difference at the same interface rather than adding an unrelated centered approximation of $-gh\nabla b$. The method is intentionally the more diffusive, first-order reference: it provides a finite-volume, well-balanced baseline against which the higher-order shallow-water labels can be compared.

\paragraph{MUSCL-HR reference.}
MUSCL-HR reconstructs $h$, $\eta = h + b$, $u$, and $v$ independently in each coordinate direction. For a scalar cell field $q$, the production limiter is the componentwise minmod slope
\[
s_i = \operatorname{minmod}(q_i - q_{i - 1},\, q_{i + 1} - q_i),
\qquad
q_i ^ \pm = q_i \pm\frac{1}{2}s_i.
\]
Slopes are set to zero when the one-sided differences change sign, suppressing new extrema; consequently, the scheme is second order in smooth monotone regions but locally reverts toward first order near extrema or sharp changes. Reconstructed depths are clipped to be nonnegative, face velocities are bounded by the same safety limit used by the quality policy, and face bathymetry is recovered consistently as $b ^ \pm = \eta ^ \pm - h ^ \pm$. Hydrostatic reconstruction and the same Rusanov flux are then applied to these face states \citep{clain2016secondorder,hou2015muscl}.

Each Euler stage also includes the reconstructed-bed momentum source
\[
S_{hu} = -\frac{g}{2}(h_E + h_W)\frac{b_E - b_W}{\Delta x},
\qquad
S_{hv} = -\frac{g}{2}(h_N + h_S)\frac{b_N - b_S}{\Delta y},
\]
where the directional states are the limited reconstructions associated with the current cell. This term is applied before the Heun stage average and works with the hydrostatic face corrections to retain the intended topographic balance.

Time integration uses the two-stage Heun from. If $\Phi_{\Delta t}(U)$ denotes one limited finite-volume Euler update, then
\[
U ^ {(1)} = \Phi_{\Delta t}(U ^ n),\qquad
U ^ {n + 1} = \frac{1}{2} U ^ n +
\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{\Delta t}\!\left(U ^ {(1)}\right).
\]
Positivity and dry-momentum projections are checked after the stage updates and after the final average. Thus MUSCL-HR differs from the primary reference in both its piecewise-linear spatial representation and its two-stage temporal update; it is not merely the same solver evaluated at a larger CFL value.

\paragraph{Weakly dispersive Boussinesq comparison.}
The Boussinesq comparison evolves surface elevation and its time derivative, $[\eta, \eta_t] ^ \top$, rather than depth and discharge. Its linear variable-depth form solves
\[
\left(I - \alpha_B \nabla \cdot(H ^ 2 \nabla)\right) a_\eta = g \nabla \cdot(H \nabla \eta),
\]
where
\[
H(x, y) = \max\!\left\{(-b(x, y) + \eta_{\mathrm{sea}}) s_H, H_{\min}\right\}.
\]
We use $\alpha_B = 1/3$, $s_H = 1$, and $H_{\min} = 10 ^ {-4}$. Spatial gradients and variable-coefficient divergences are represented by centered second-order finite differences with depth coefficients averaged to cell faces. For fixed bathymetry, the dispersive mass operator on the left-hand side is unchanged throughout a rollout. Its face coefficients and sparse factorization are therefore cached, and each matrix-free conjugate-gradient acceleration solve uses the cached sparse-LU factor as a preconditioner. The relative residual tolerance is $10 ^ {-10}$ with at most $750$ iterations per solve.

Two acceleration solves form one velocity-Verlet step:
\[
\eta ^ {n + 1} = \eta ^ n + \Delta t\, \eta_t ^ n + \frac{1}{2}\Delta t ^ 2a_\eta ^ n,
\qquad
\eta_t ^ {n + 1} = \eta_t ^ n + \frac{\Delta t}{2} \left(a_\eta ^ n + a_\eta ^ {n + 1} \right).
\]
The production path fails the sample if either solve does not meet its residual tolerance; a nonconverged iterate is not retained as a label. For each fixed bathymetry, the natural step is selected from the maximum shallow long-wave speed,
\[
\Delta t = \frac{0.35}{\sqrt{g\max(H)}\left(\Delta x ^ {-1} + \Delta y ^ {-1}\right)}.
\]
This is an elevation-only linear weakly dispersive comparison, not a full nonlinear coastal Boussinesq model. It is included to create a controlled equation-family contrast, not because it is assumed to be more accurate than the shallow-water references.

\paragraph{Boundaries, damping, and shared scope.}
The shallow-water references use bathymetry-aware linearized radiation states at all four outer edges. The outgoing free-surface characteristic is continued through the face around the local rest depth, while the tangential velocity is carried from the adjacent interior cell. This treatment reduces direct reflection for small outgoing waves but is not an exact non-reflecting condition for nonlinear, oblique, or strongly heterogeneous propagation. The Boussinesq comparison instead uses open zero-gradient scalar boundaries.

All three references additionally use the same 16-cell cosine sponge in the outer computational buffer. If $D(x, y) \in [0.8, 1]$ is the reference mask and $\Delta t_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0.0035$, a natural step applies $D ^ {\Delta t/\Delta t_{\mathrm{ref}}}$. Consequently, two trajectories covering the same elapsed time receive approximately the same cumulative damping even when their natural step sizes or counts differ. The shallow-water sponge damps momentum and free-surface displacement about rest depth; the Boussinesq sponge damps $\eta$ and $\eta_t$. The Boussinesq production filter is disabled. Across all three references, the benchmark omits Coriolis acceleration, bottom friction, moving bathymetry, and event-specific forcing after initialization.

\subsection{Numerical verification and validation scope}
\label{subsec:reference-validation}

The numerical-label contract was assessed before the final surrogate analysis through five complementary forms of evidence:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
    \item \textbf{Input identity:} all $13{,}500$ scenario definitions were checked for split-qualified identity, array equality, horizon coverage, and exact reuse across the three references.
    \item \textbf{Internal verification:} analytical-wave, lake-at-rest, conservation, spatial/temporal sensitivity, requested-output, boundary/sponge, dispersive, health, and deterministic-replay checks were run with thresholds fixed before evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Independent comparison:} matched analytical and small production-like cases were compared with GeoClaw/Clawpack. The analytical and refinement checks passed, but most exploratory production-like comparisons exceeded their descriptive limits. The external solver is therefore an independent comparator, not automatic truth or evidence of pointwise equivalence.
    \item \textbf{Generation-path checks:} the exact buffered-domain, adaptive-step, interpolation, and quality-screening path was exercised on cases separate from the implementation checks.
    \item \textbf{Independent sensitivity set:} additional training-pool cases, excluded from the earlier diagnostic selections, tested the fixed production/reference CFL pairs, time extraction, health criteria, and replay consistency.
\end{enumerate}

Decision thresholds and case selections were fixed before the corresponding outcomes were inspected. Validation and test scenarios used to evaluate the surrogates were not used to tune these numerical gates. The full chain was repeated under a fixed source revision before the reported model evaluation. Passing it supports the implemented finite-horizon label contract; it does not establish observed-event validity, shoreline accuracy, generally non-reflecting boundaries, or equivalence with an operational tsunami code.

Each mass-generation rollout must additionally pass finiteness, positivity/depth, amplitude, velocity, sanitization, projection, clipping, post-step CFL, and, where relevant, conjugate-gradient checks. Rejected or incompatible outputs are not silently reused.

\subsection{Field and physics metrics}
\label{subsec:eval-metrics}

For field accuracy, with $k$ indexing every element of the trajectory,
\[
\mathrm{MAE} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_k | \widehat{y}_k - y_k |,
\qquad
\mathrm{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_k(\widehat{y}_k - y_k) ^ 2},
\]
\[
\mathrm{rel}\, L_2 = \frac{\|\widehat{y} - y \|_2}{\| y \|_2},
\qquad
\mathrm{max\text{-}err}=\max_k | \widehat{y}_k - y_k |.
\]
MAE, RMSE, and the headline relative $L_2$ are computed after reversing target normalization. Main-table max-err remains in normalized target units unless marked physical.

For a full split, the underlying sums are accumulated before reduction, giving one global relative $L_2$ and one global maximum, Metadata subgroup tables instead compute per-scenario metrics first and then average within the group. These two aggregations answer different questions and are labelled separately.

For the selected single-seed Hydrostatic architecture comparison, we also use a paired nonparametric bootstrap: one shared index draw resamples the same $2{,}500$ scenario identities $2{,}000$ times with seed $20260814$. We report mean per-scenario denormalized relative $L_2$ and paired differences from FNO. These are test-scenario intervals for fixed checkpoints, not training-seed uncertainty or population-level architecture rankings.

Per-frame relative $L_2$ uses the same norm ratio after restricting the tensors to one requested time. Physics diagnostics additionally use the spatial means
\[
\overline{\eta}(t) = \frac{1}{HW}\sum_{i, j}\eta_{i, j}(t),
\qquad
\overline{h}(t) = \frac{1}{HW}\sum_{i, j}\bigl(\eta_{i, j}(t) - b_{i, j}\bigr),
\]
where $\overline{h}$ is a fully wet mass proxy rather than an independently conserved model output. Spectral error is computed from orthonormal two-dimensional FFT coefficients in radial wavenumber bands $\kappa \leq 8$, $8 < \kappa \leq 16$, and $\kappa > 16$:
\[
E_{\mathcal{B}} = \frac{\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{F}(\widehat{Y} - Y)\|_2}{\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{F}(Y)\|_2}.
\]
These diagnostics localize error in integral and spectral structure; none of the neural models enforces a conservation law.

\subsection{Generalization and resolution transfer}
\label{subsec:generalization-protocol}

Metadata-filtered suites select \texttt{multi-gauss}, \texttt{trench}, or source strength $s \ge 0.82$ from the ordinary test set. These are difficult subgroups within the training support, not unseen-family OOD datasets. The strict tests remove one bathymetry or source family before preprocessing and training, refit normalization on the filtered training set, and evaluate the excluded family separately. When a checkpoint is applied to data standardized with another training distribution, the inputs are first restored with the dataset statistics and then re-standardized with the checkpoint's training statistics. Predictions and targets are finally compared after target denormalization in one benchmark-scale space.

The proxy resolution diagnostic resizes the processed Hydrostatic test fields to $32 \times 32$ or $128 \times 128$ and applies the same $64 \times 64$ checkpoint. The native experiment instead uses paired MUSCL-HR simulations at $32$, $64$, and $128$ resolution. Three checkpoints are trained separately, and the full train-grid/test-grid matrix is evaluated. Each target dataset is rebased into the checkpoint's training normalization before inference.

\subsection{Runtime benchmarking}
\label{subsec:runtime}

The numerical references are timed on CPU using the exact common-time $96 \times 96$ rollout and central-crop contract, excluding bathymetry and source generation. These are vectorized NumPy/SciPy implementations. Each reference timing uses eight fixed scenarios, one warm-up pass, and three measured repeats. Surrogates are timed on the RTX 4050 Laptop GPU in fp32 at batch size one, with TF32 disabled, five warm-up passes, twenty measured passes, and device synchronization around timing. The implementation-level speedup is
\[
\mathrm{speedup} =
\frac{\text{reference rollout time per sample}}
    {\text{surrogate inference time per sample}}.
\]
Loading the stored scenario, preparing the buffered inputs, and constructing the solver are recorded separately from the rollout denominator. The comparison therefore measures the repeated forward task under stated hardware, not end-to-end hazard-system latency. The denominator is not a compiled, GPU, adaptive-mesh, or operational tsunami solver.

\subsection{Cross-reference discrepancy}
\label{subsec:cross-solver}

The raw solver gap compares two numerical references on the same scenarios and requested times. A second diagnostic compares a surrogate trained on reference $A$ with reference $B$:
\[
\rho(A \rightarrow B) = 
\frac{\mathrm{RMSE}(f_\theta ^ A, \mathrm{ref}\, B)}
     {\mathrm{RMSE}(\mathrm{ref}\, A, \mathrm{ref}\, B)}.
\]
All terms use the global denormalized benchmark-scale RMSE, and confidence intervals use 2,000 scenario bootstrap resamples. A value $\rho < 1$ means that the surrogate is closer to reference $B$ in this benchmark metric than reference $A$ is. Without observational truth or a stronger physical reference, this is not evidence of physical superiority.

\subsection{Gauge, arrival, and peak diagnostics}
\label{subsec:wave-metrics}

We evaluate nine fixed virtual gauges arranged on a $3 \times 3$ interior grid. For target waveform $y_g(t_k)$ and prediction $\widehat{y}_g(t_k)$, the waveform normalized RMSE is
\[
\mathrm{NRMSE}_g = \frac{\sqrt{T ^ {-1} \sum_k \bigl(\widehat{y}_g(t_k) - y_g(t_k) \bigr) ^ 2}}{\max_k | y_g(t_k) |}.
\]
Gauges with target amplitude at or below $10 ^ {-12}$ are excluded from waveform NRMSE and relative-peak summaries and counted as inactive, avoiding division by a numerically zero target. The gauge-arrival threshold is $10\%$ of the target gauge's maximum absolute amplitude and is never changed by the prediction. A target gauge that never crosses the threshold is inactive; one already active at $t_1$ is excluded because the true arrival occurred before the observed window. An eligible gauge is compared only if the prediction also crosses the threshold, and missing predicted arrivals are counted separately. Peak elevation is the maximum signed surface value. To reduce one-frame instability in the time-to-peak metric, all times within $1\%$ of the target or predicted peak-to-trough range are treated as a plateau and averaged.

A separate arrival-map diagnostic uses a single target-only threshold equal to $10\%$ of the sample-wide maximum absolute target elevation, applies it at every pixel, and reports requested-frame index differences only where both prediction and target cross. Its eligibility fraction is reported together with the error. The summary first computes mean absolute index error over the eligible pixels of each scenario and then averages those scenario values; it does not pool all eligible pixels into one global reduction. These are normalized-domain virtual-observation diagnostics, not comparisons with measured coastal gauges.

\subsection{Uncertainty quantification}
\label{subsec:uncertainty}

A seven-member FNO ensemble differs only in seed. For member predictions $\widehat{Y} ^ {(m)}$,
\[
\bar{Y} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m = 1} ^ {M}\widehat{Y} ^ {(m)},
\qquad
\sigma ^ 2 = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m = 1} ^ {M}\left(\widehat{Y} ^ {(m)} - \bar{Y}\right) ^ 2.
\]
For a central Gaussian interval with nominal coverage $p$, we use
\[
\bar{Y}\pm z_p\sigma,
\qquad
z_p = \sqrt{2}\, \operatorname{erf} ^ {-1}(p).
\]
We report empirical coverage and the Pearson correlation between $\sigma$ and $| \bar{Y} - Y |$. Because the raw ensemble is under-dispersed, one scalar variance factor is estimated on validation elements only:
\[
\gamma = \max\!\left\{1,\, \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{val}}} \sum_k\frac{(Y_k - \bar{Y}_k) ^ 2}{\max(\sigma_k ^ 2, \varepsilon)} \right\}.
\]
The calibrated interval replaces $\sigma$ by $\sqrt{\gamma}\sigma$ and applies the same factor unchanged to test and subgroup suites. Coverage is marginal over correlated pixels and frames, so these are interval-scale reliability diagnostics rather than calibrated event probabilities.
