\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Main findings}
The central result is that numerical reference discrepancies can be smaller than, comparable to, or larger than selected surrogate errors depending on the reference pair and metric. The evaluation uses the same requested output times for each reference while retaining its natural time steps, so that the reported discrepancies compare fields at the same elapsed times. Interpolation error remains part of the numerical approximation.

The smooth nested-grid and periodic-wave checks show solver-specific refinement behavior. Hydrostatic approaches first order in the periodic test, while MUSCL-HR discrepancy levels off at fine grids. Because the numerical equations are nonlinear and the comparison wave is linearized, this plateau includes finite-amplitude effects and cannot uniquely measure discretization error. These bounded tests do not establish formal convergence or event-scale accuracy.

The full-horizon study finds contracting grid differences on six cases, with boundary and damping sensitivity larger than half-CFL sensitivity. Differences peak before the final frame, so final-time error alone misses part of the discrepancy. Lake-at-rest drift remains near roundoff, while moving-equilibrium drift is nonzero and solver-dependent. Fixed raster inputs and finite selections limit the interpretation.

The matched GeoClaw refinement also bounds what self-differences imply. Hydrostatic moves closer to the fine comparator in all three cases, whereas MUSCL-HR moves farther away in the trench/rough case. Contraction of differences between two in-house grids therefore does not by itself establish uniformly improving agreement with an independent code. For fixed neural predictions, changing the Hydrostatic reference grid increases measured relative error by up to $5.08\%$ and changes some case-level model orderings, even though the six-case aggregate ordering is stable. This supplies a concrete consequence of reference-aware evaluation: numerical-reference sensitivity should accompany small reported model differences, with aggregate and case-level effects distinguished.

ConvLSTM, U-FNO, F-FNO, and FNO retain their mean per-scenario ordering at every replicated seed. This supports stability for that subset, while the selected global table and scenario bootstrap address different aggregations and uncertainty sources. First-frame-seeded window models solve an easier conditional task and remain separate.

\subsection{Reference choice and numerical comparison}
At identical requested times, the global RMSE gaps are $0.00171$ for Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR, $0.00305$ for MUSCL-HR--Boussinesq, and $0.00374$ for Hydrostatic--Boussinesq. The six seed-$18$ cross-reference ratios range from $1.10$ to $1.72$ and all exceed one, so no tested emulator bridges its raw solver gap in this metric. These ratios remain benchmark-relative effects rather than evidence of physical convergence.

The baseline GeoClaw comparison retains mean relative-$L_2$ discrepancies of $0.2599$ and $0.1256$ for Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR. Matched initial fields and output times leave discretization, time integration, and boundaries as unresolved contributors. The earlier normalized-domain ablation uses different inputs and cannot establish main-benchmark accuracy or identify physical error \citep{leveque2011tsunami,berger2011geoclaw}.

The pooled-reference comparison gives a complementary answer to whether a shared surrogate simply averages the references. Without solver identity, the model produces a compromise output whose nearest reference is most often Hydrostatic, followed by MUSCL-HR and Boussinesq. With the requested solver identity, mean target-specific RMSE decreases by approximately $18\%$, $8\%$, and $19\%$ for Hydrostatic, MUSCL-HR, and Boussinesq, respectively. Thus conditioning preserves solver-specific structure and improves agreement with the selected numerical reference, but the result is not a recovery of physical truth and does not universally bridge the raw solver gaps.

\subsection{Model and transfer behavior}
Architecture size alone does not explain accuracy: F-FNO is much smaller than FNO, and increasing FNO modes from $14$ to $20$ gives no improvement. ConvLSTM has the lowest low- and mid-frequency errors among the diagnostic models; F-FNO improves low-frequency agreement but has larger mid- and high-frequency errors.

On ten fully wet GEBCO-derived crops independently rescaled to the training depth range and paired with synthetic sources, direct F-FNO has lower error than direct FNO. This supports morphology transfer under the benchmark construction, not real-event, physical-depth, shoreline, or wet--dry generalization. Rough-source, resolution-transfer, and wet--dry stress tests expose additional limits.

\subsection{Uncertainty and practical scope}
The ensemble is seed-stable but under-dispersed. Validation-based inflation raises marginal test coverage near its nominal level, yet simultaneous coverage is $0.8872$ overall and $0.6780$ for high-strength sources, with broad bands. Lower mean predicted spread also fails to identify scenarios with lower relative error. Thus marginal coverage, subgroup reliability, and scenario-level error ranking require separate assessment. Validation reuse for checkpoint selection further prevents an independent-calibration guarantee.

These results support a controlled finite-horizon surrogate comparison and expose architecture-, reference-, and subgroup-dependent error behavior. Deployment would additionally require validated dimensional scaling, surveyed bathymetry, wet--dry coastal physics, and calibrated hazard-scale uncertainty quantification.
