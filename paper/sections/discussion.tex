\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Main findings}
The central result is that numerical reference discrepancies can match or exceed reported surrogate errors. Our reference-aware temporal alignment protocol establishes a shared output timeline without modifying the underlying learning task, preventing solver-specific natural time steps from corrupting performance evaluation.

Across seeds 18, 36, and 67, the checkpoint-level mean per-scenario Hydrostatic relative-$L_2$ ordering is $\mathrm{F\text{-}FNO} < \mathrm{ConvLSTM} < \mathrm{FNO} < \mathrm{U\text{-}Net}$. This is a descriptive stability result for the replicated subset, not a population-level architecture ranking. ConvLSTM incorporates explicit temporal feedback at higher computational cost, while the windowed models solve an easier first-frame-conditioned task and are not like-for-like direct forecasts.

\subsection{Reference choice and numerical comparison}
At identical evaluation times, the global RMSE gap between Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR ($0.00508$) is 1.6 times the Hydrostatic FNO error and 2.1 times the Hydrostatic F-FNO error. The two shallow-water--Boussinesq gaps ($\mathrm{RMSE} \approx 0.045$) exceed the same-reference Boussinesq FNO error by more than fivefold. Across the three replicated FNO seeds, neither shallow-water direction bridges the Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR gap. The near-unity cross-family ratios are small, direction-dependent benchmark effects rather than evidence of physical convergence.

In the three prespecified representative cases, halving the in-house cell size is the dominant tested sensitivity and reduces all six discrepancies, whereas the tested sponge and boundary changes have small effects. A material residual remains; this is not a formal convergence study, and GeoClaw is an independent comparator rather than physical truth.

\subsection{Model and transfer behavior}
F-FNO has lower Hydrostatic error with fewer parameters than FNO in the tested runs, but performance does not strictly scale with spectral capacity: increasing retained modes from 14 to 20 gives only a slight improvement, while the eight-mode variant performs marginally better. ConvLSTM has lower late-time error than direct FNO but higher wavenumber error, whereas F-FNO has the lowest reported low-frequency spectral error.

On ten fully wet GEBCO-derived crops independently rescaled to the training depth range and paired with synthetic sources, direct F-FNO has lower error than direct FNO. This supports morphology transfer under the benchmark construction, not real-event, physical-depth, shoreline, or wet--dry generalization. Rough-source, resolution-transfer, and wet--dry stress tests expose additional limits.

\subsection{Uncertainty and practical scope}
The seven-member FNO ensemble provides stable mean trajectory forecasts but suffers from under-dispersion. Validation-fitted scalar inflation improves elementwise coverage, yet high-strength wave subgroups remain under-covered and error--uncertainty correlations remain moderate. Because coverage metrics pool spatially and temporally correlated pixels, they serve as interval-scale reliability indicators rather than event-level probability calibrators.

In summary, under the stated laptop-GPU benchmark, the emulators achieve millisecond-scale trajectory generation on complex wavefields. Operational deployment would additionally require validated dimensional scaling, surveyed bathymetry, wet--dry coastal physics, and calibrated hazard-scale uncertainty quantification.
