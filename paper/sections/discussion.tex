\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Main findings}
The central result is that the numerical reference and its temporal-output protocol can contribute an error scale comparable to, or larger than, the surrogate error being reported. The common-time construction does not change the basic machine-learning task, but it gives every label one shared temporal meaning and prevents solver-specific natural step counts from defining incompatible targets. This reference-aware separation is a methodological control rather than an architecture contribution, yet it materially changes which scientific claims the benchmark can support.

The established neural architectures serve as controlled demonstrations. Across seeds $18$, $36$, and $67$, the Hydrostatic mean per-scenario relative-$L_2$ ordering remains F-FNO, ConvLSTM, FNO, then U-Net. This strengthens the main direct comparison beyond one initialization, although it does not replicate every baseline. The broader result is still a speed--accuracy--conditioning trade-off: ConvLSTM is recurrent and comparatively slow, while the lower-error windowed models receive the first requested target frame and therefore solve an easier conditional task.

\subsection{Reference choice and numerical comparison}
Once the outputs are compared at identical requested times, Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR remain the closest pair. Their global RMSE gap, $0.00508$, is nevertheless about $1.6$ times the same-reference Hydrostatic FNO RMSE and about $2.1$ times the F-FNO RMSE. Thus even within the shallow-water family, the choice of numerical reference is not a small perturbation relative to the model error. The two shallow-water--Boussinesq gaps are much larger still: their RMSE values near $0.045$ are more than five times the Boussinesq FNO's same-reference RMSE.

The three-seed cross-reference ratios sharpen that interpretation. Both shallow-water directions remain above one for every seed, so neither FNO bridges the Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR gap. Hydrostatic $\rightarrow$ Boussinesq remains essentially equal to one; MUSCL-HR $\rightarrow$ Boussinesq is only slightly below one; and both Boussinesq-origin directions change sides across seeds. Smoothing or bias can therefore move a surrogate marginally closer to another reference in a global norm without recovering a more accurate physical solution. Without observations or a demonstrably stronger reference, ``closer to another solver'' and ``more physically accurate'' remain different statements.

The GeoClaw ablation further narrows, but does not eliminate, the numerical uncertainty. Removing the sponge, changing the outer boundary, or moving it farther away has little effect in the three prespecified representative cases. Halving the in-house cell size improves all six solver--case comparisons and is the dominant tested sensitivity, yet substantial residual discrepancies remain. MUSCL-HR is closer than Hydrostatic in every tested configuration, but those solvers differ jointly in reconstruction, source treatment, time integration, and CFL. The evidence therefore supports resolution sensitivity rather than one isolated implementation cause, formal convergence, or GeoClaw as physical truth. Reported model errors remain emulation errors against explicit numerical references.

\subsection{Model and transfer behavior}
The direct ranking is broadly consistent with the inductive-bias argument of Section~\ref{sec:models}, but it does not reduce to ``spectral is always better.'' F-FNO combines the lowest replicated Hydrostatic error with far fewer parameters than FNO, while ConvLSTM benefits from explicit temporal feedback. Axis-wise factorization may provide useful structural regularization, but that mechanism is not isolated here. Increasing the retained FNO modes from $14$ to $20$ does not improve the selected-seed result, and the eight-mode ablation is slightly better than the base model; spectral bandwidth alone is therefore not the evident limiting factor.

The temporal and spectral diagnostics expose complementary errors. ConvLSTM has lower late-time error than direct FNO but larger high-wavenumber error, whereas F-FNO most clearly improves low-frequency and integral behavior. Rough sources remain difficult in both subgroup and strict-holdout tests. The present experiments do not separate spectral truncation, training density, source semantics, and optimization as possible causes.

The Boussinesq results illustrate why metrics must remain reference-specific. Its FNO has the lowest same-reference relative $L_2$ among the three FNO targets but the largest denormalized RMSE, because the target norms and waveforms differ. Its large waveform-error tail and small arrival-eligible subset also prevent the low arrival-map error from supporting a superior-arrival claim. The solver is useful as a deliberately different weakly dispersive label family, not as an automatic fidelity upgrade over the shallow-water references.

Generalization remains conditional on the test construction. Strict family removal exposes larger continental and rough-source failures than in-distribution subgroup analysis, but also changes training diversity and normalization. Proxy and native resolution studies both degrade away from their training setup without establishing a resolution-scaling law. Direct F-FNO transfers better than direct FNO to the ten fully wet GEBCO-derived crops, while the coastline stress failure shows that offshore morphology transfer does not imply shoreline or wet--dry skill.

\subsection{Uncertainty and practical scope}
The seven-member FNO ensemble is stable in mean error but under-dispersed. Validation-only scalar inflation brings average elementwise coverage closer to nominal, yet the high-strength subgroup remains under-covered and the error--uncertainty correlation is only moderate. Because coverage pools strongly correlated pixels and frames, it diagnoses interval scale rather than the probability that a complete event or hazard is contained.

The benchmark is consequently a controlled study of surrogate behavior, not an operational forecasting system. Once the numerical reference and temporal-output protocol are fixed, several models emulate 50-frame trajectories in milliseconds and retain useful accuracy on fully wet unseen morphologies. Operational use would additionally require physical units, realistic rupture models, surveyed bathymetry, friction and wet--dry physics, validated boundaries, observed gauges, optimized solver comparisons, and event-level uncertainty assessment.
