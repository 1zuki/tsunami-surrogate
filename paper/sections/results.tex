\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Unless stated otherwise, model accuracy is measured on the $N = 2500$ held-out test scenarios. MAE, RMSE, and headline relative $L_2$ values are computed after reversing target normalization; max-err remains in normalized target units. Every table uses the same completed common-time evaluation and the checkpoint, dataset, and metric definitions stated in Sections~\ref{sec:methods}--\ref{sec:training}.

\subsection{Temporal alignment and numerical verification}
\label{subsec:res-dataset}

All three main datasets contain $10{,}000$ training, $1{,}000$ validation, and $2{,}500$ test scenarios. Each processed target has shape $50 \times 64 \times 64$, and all $40{,}500$ solver-specific trajectories satisfy the predefined numerical-stability and solution-quality criteria. The scenario populations match across references: six source families, five bathymetry families, and source strength in $[0.45, 0.85]$.

Table~\ref{tab:solver-validation} summarizes the numerical evidence supporting the finite-horizon temporal-output protocol. The categories are grouped by their scientific purpose.

Removing the sponge, changing the boundary type, and moving the boundary outward alter the mean discrepancy by only $0.0003$--$0.0032$. Halving the cell size moves all six solver--case comparisons closer to GeoClaw and reduces the mean discrepancy by $0.1451$ for Hydrostatic and $0.1010$ for MUSCL-HR, making refinement the dominant tested sensitivity. A material residual remains, however, and MUSCL-HR is closer in every tested setup. Because the Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR contrast changes reconstruction, source treatment, time integration, and CFL together, the ablation neither identifies one unique cause nor establishes formal convergence or pointwise equivalence.

The linear weakly dispersive implementation also reproduces its discrete constant-depth phase-speed trend over eight Fourier modes. The maximum relative error is $2.45 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ for the non-dispersive branch and $1.91 \times 10 ^ {-8}$ for $\alpha_B = 1 / 3$, with no conjugate-gradient failures and at most $35$ iterations in the latter branch (Table~\ref{tab:app-bouss-dispersion}). This is a local implementation diagnostic rather than external or event-scale validation.

The central evaluation result is that the remaining reference discrepancy is not small relative to surrogate error. As quantified in Section~\ref{subsec:res-solver-fidelity}, the common-time Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR RMSE gap is $1.6$ times the Hydrostatic FNO error and $2.1$ times the F-FNO error, while either shallow-water--Boussinesq gap exceeds the same-reference Boussinesq FNO error by more than five times. The architecture comparisons below therefore demonstrate the consequences of the reference-aware protocol rather than defining its primary contribution.

\paragraph{Paired scenario uncertainty.}
The paired scenario bootstrap gives mean per-scenario relative $L_2$ values of $0.222$ ($95\%$ interval $[0.218, 0.226]$) for F-FNO, $0.272$ ($[0.266, 0.278]$) for ConvLSTM, and $0.304$ ($[0.298, 0.310]$) for FNO. Relative to FNO, the paired mean differences are $-0.082$ ($[-0.085, -0.079]$) for F-FNO and $-0.032$ ($[-0.036, -0.028]$) for ConvLSTM. Their per-scenario errors are lower than FNO on $93.2\%$ and $64.0\%$ of the test scenarios, respectively. These intervals describe variation over the fixed test scenarios for the selected checkpoints; they are not multi-seed significance claims.

\subsection{Gauge, arrival, and peak diagnostics}
\label{subsec:res-wave}

Boussinesq has a much larger waveform tail and far fewer arrival-eligible gauges: $22{,}132$ of $22{,}500$ target gauges are already active at the first requested time, and two additional eligible gauges have no predicted threshold crossing. This is an arrival-eligibility effect rather than a shortage of virtual gauges: the threshold is defined from each target gauge's own waveform, and any gauge already above it at the first supervised frame has an arrival before the observable target window. \subsection{Ensemble uncertainty and seed stability}
\label{subsec:res-uncertainty}

Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty} summarizes the seven-member ensemble reliability. The raw ensemble is under-dispersed: on the ordinary test split, coverage is $0.419$, $0.654$, $0.742$, and $0.798$ for nominal $50\%$, $80\%$, $90\%$, and $95\%$ intervals. The error--uncertainty correlation is $0.562$, indicating useful but only moderate spatial ranking.

A validation-only variance multiplier $\gamma = 3.981$ (standard-deviation scale $1.995$) raises test $90\%$ coverage from $0.742$ to $0.920$. It also improves the multi-Gaussian subgroup from $0.664$ to $0.888$ and trench from $0.748$ to $0.924$, while the high-strength tail remains under-covered at $0.821$.

Together with the moderate error--uncertainty correlation, this supports using ensemble spread as a relative warning signal within the benchmark rather than as a calibrated hazard probability.
