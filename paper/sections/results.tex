\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Unless stated otherwise, model accuracy is measured on the $N = 2500$ held-out test scenarios. MAE, RMSE, and headline relative $L_2$ values are computed after reversing target normalization; max-err remains in normalized target units. The main model tables use the validated common-time evaluation; numerical-comparator studies and selected failure-case analyses are reported under the model-selection, dataset, and metric definitions stated in Sections~\ref{sec:methods}--\ref{sec:training}.

\subsection{Numerical accuracy and reference validation}
\label{subsec:res-dataset}

All three main datasets contain $10{,}000$ training, $1{,}000$ validation, and $2{,}500$ test scenarios. Each processed target has shape $50 \times 64 \times 64$, and all $40{,}500$ solver-specific trajectories satisfy the predefined quality criteria. The scenario populations match across references: six source families, five bathymetry families, and resolved source strength in approximately $[0.075, 0.300]$ after the initial-elevation-to-depth cap.

Section~\ref{subsec:reference-validation} defines the numerical studies; Appendix~\ref{app:validation-details} retains supporting checks. The results below assess finite-horizon sensitivity rather than event-scale physical validity.

The independent-comparator evidence is deliberately limited in scope. Table~\ref{tab:geoclaw-ablation} compares the two shallow-water references with GeoClaw on three fixed test cases using the same $192 \times 192$ computational grid, paired initial fields, and 50 requested times from $8.4$ to $420$. The central $128 \times 128$ fields are reduced by cell averaging to $64 \times 64$ for comparison. GeoClaw uses open extrapolation boundaries, whereas the benchmark references retain their radiation boundaries and sponge. These results therefore measure inter-code discrepancy under the stated settings; they do not establish pointwise equivalence or dataset-wide numerical accuracy. The earlier boundary and refinement ablation used a different grid and scenario construction and is reported separately in Appendix~\ref{app:legacy-geoclaw-ablation}.

MUSCL-HR is closer to GeoClaw in all three cases, but both references retain material discrepancies. The maximum initial-state mismatch is $8.88 \times 10 ^ {-16}$, and the maximum requested-time mismatch is $5.69 \times 10 ^ {-14}$, so these comparisons are not explained by a mismatched initial state or output schedule. Boundary treatment, discretization, and time integration still differ. The three cases cover only three bathymetry--source combinations and cannot establish a population-wide accuracy ranking or a unique cause of the remaining discrepancy.

Doubling the GeoClaw grid to $384 \times 384$ on the same physical domain gives coarse--fine RMSEs of $3.86 \times 10 ^ {-5}$--$9.42 \times 10 ^ {-5}$ after restriction to the common output grid. Using the finer comparator increases RMSE against the archived benchmark fields in all six comparisons, by $0.006\%$--$3.523\%$. The remaining inter-code discrepancy is therefore much larger than this tested comparator-grid sensitivity, although neither GeoClaw grid is an exact solution (Appendix~\ref{app:geoclaw-grid-sensitivity}).

Refining the in-house computations gives a different result from refining GeoClaw alone. Against the fixed $384 \times 384$ GeoClaw comparator, Hydrostatic RMSE decreases by $20.3\%$--$25.2\%$ in all three cases when its core is refined from $128$ to $256$ cells. MUSCL-HR improves by $4.48\%$ and $2.16\%$ on the continental/Gaussian and seamounts/fault cases, but worsens by $17.34\%$ on trench/rough (Table~\ref{tab:matched-geoclaw-refinement}). Mean trajectory relative $L_2$ against this comparator changes from $0.2608$ to $0.1996$ for Hydrostatic and from $0.1265$ to $0.1270$ for MUSCL-HR. Thus the remaining discrepancy does not decrease uniformly with in-house refinement. The boundary treatments still differ, and neither grid supplies physical truth.

The full-horizon study comprised $92$ rollouts over six fixed cases, three boundary-control cases, five lake-at-rest cases, and two moving-equilibrium resolutions. All rollouts remained finite. The minimum recorded water depth was $0.7413$ benchmark units, the largest post-step CFL was $0.2251$, and no NaN replacement, positivity or dry-state projection, or velocity clipping was recorded. For the six cases with nested grids, the median trajectory RMSE from $64 \rightarrow 128$ was $7.32 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ for Hydrostatic and $4.55 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ for MUSCL-HR; from $128 \rightarrow 256$ it was $4.66 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ and $2.00 \times 10 ^ {-4}$, respectively. For both solvers, the baseline--fine difference was smaller than the coarse--baseline difference in every case. The median Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR discrepancy decreased from $1.46 \times 10 ^ {-3}$ at the $64$-cell core to $1.16 \times 10 ^ {-3}$ at $128$ and $7.27 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ at $256$. Figure~\ref{fig:full-horizon-numerical-study} shows these paired comparisons. These are fixed-raster, combined space--time differences, not formal convergence orders.

The median difference between the common-CFL and half-CFL runs was $2.99 \times 10 ^ {-5}$ for Hydrostatic and $7.87 \times 10 ^ {-7}$ for MUSCL-HR. Removing the sponge changed the baseline trajectories by median RMSEs of $4.12 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ and $4.79 \times 10 ^  {-4}$, replacing radiation with open boundaries changed them by $5.86 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ and $6.32 \times 10 ^ {-4}$, and extending the no-sponge exterior changed them by $2.76 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ and $3.25 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ for Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR, respectively. The median $128 \rightarrow 256$ per-time difference peaked near the middle of the horizon at $5.69 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ for Hydrostatic and $2.96 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ for MUSCL-HR before declining to $1.56 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ and $8.00 \times 10 ^ {-5}$ at $t = 420$. Full-domain water-volume and mechanical-energy changes were recorded as diagnostics; across the six baseline cases, peak absolute water-volume changes from the initial state ranged from $4.87 \times 10 ^ 3$--$1.21 \times 10 ^ 5$ and $5.68 \times 10 ^ 3$--$1.24 \times 10 ^ 5$, and the corresponding peak absolute mechanical-energy changes per unit density ranged from $5.82 \times 10 ^ 2$--$1.79 \times 10 ^ 4$ and $5.82 \times 10 ^ 2$--$1.79 \times 10 ^ 4$ for Hydrostatic and MUSCL-HR. Open boundaries and damping prevent interpreting these values as closed-system conservation errors.

The full-horizon lake-at-rest runs over five bathymetry families had maximum free-surface drift below $2.7 \times 10 ^ {-15}$ and maximum velocity below $1.8 \times 10 ^ {-14}$; the largest discharge drift was $1.75 \times 10 ^ {-13}$. The moving-equilibrium test retained nonzero drift: at the two resolutions, Hydrostatic elevation and discharge drifts were $2.21 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ and $1.00 \times 10 ^ {-3}$ at $128$ cells and $1.12 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ and $5.34 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ at $256$ cells, whereas MUSCL-HR gave $6.61 \times 10 ^ {-6}$ and $2.67 \times 10 ^ {-5}$ and then $1.75 \times 10 ^ {-6}$ and $6.74 \times 10 ^ {-6}$. These stationary and moving tests support solver-specific empirical statements under the stated setup; they do not establish general well-balancedness or physical accuracy.

The earlier nested-grid self-convergence study used twelve shared smooth variable-bathymetry scenarios. Each scenario was generated on a $128 \times 128$ master grid, restricted by cell averaging to $64 \times 64$ and $32 \times 32$, and advanced to $t = 0.02$ with adaptive stepping, reflective boundaries, and the sponge disabled. The two solvers were evaluated at CFL values $0.1125$ and $0.225$; Table~\ref{tab:self-convergence} reports the selected rows, with the alternate-CFL results in the Appendix. To complement the self-differences, a separate constant-depth periodic linear-wave diagnostic used $32$, $64$, $128$, $256$, and $512$ grids at three CFL values. On the finest grid, the relative-$L_2$ error against the linearized reference was $0.0334$--$0.0526$ for Hydrostatic and $0.007394$--$0.007396$ for MUSCL-HR. Hydrostatic approached first-order behavior through the finest pair. MUSCL-HR gave approximately $1.3$--$1.5$ order on the first two pairs, about $1.04$--$1.05$ on the $128$--$256$ pair, and about $0.29$ on the $256$--$512$ pair as discrepancy from the linearized reference leveled off. Finite-amplitude nonlinear effects contribute to this discrepancy, so its plateau cannot be assigned uniquely to discretization. This is solver-specific empirical accuracy evidence; it does not support a blanket second-order or formal convergence claim for the full solver. The full table is retained in Appendix~\ref{app:validation-details}.

A separate short component-level lake-at-rest check also covered three representative bathymetries for 32 steps with reflective boundaries and no sponge. MUSCL-HR had zero recorded free-surface drift in each case; its largest absolute velocity component was $3.11 \times 10^{-15}$ benchmark units (Appendix~\ref{app:lake-at-rest}).

Closed-domain mass-conservation cases also met all six solver--case criteria at $64 \times 64$, $t = 0.02$, and $128 \times 128$, $t = 0.05$, with reflective boundaries and no sponge. The maximum relative mass drift was $1.97 \times 10^{-16}$ and $1.11 \times 10^{-16}$, respectively. These results describe conservation in a closed domain; they do not provide an open-boundary mass or flux budget.

The linear weakly dispersive reference also reproduces its discrete constant-depth phase-speed trend over eight Fourier modes. The maximum relative error is $2.45 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ for the non-dispersive branch and $1.91 \times 10 ^ {-8}$ for $\alpha_B = 1 / 3$, with no conjugate-gradient failures and at most $35$ iterations in the latter branch (Table~\ref{tab:app-bouss-dispersion}). This is a local numerical analysis rather than external or event-scale validation.

Together, these analyses separate numerical accuracy evidence from reference identity and evaluation procedure. The central model result is that reference discrepancy and surrogate error are on the same benchmark scale, but their ordering depends on the reference pair and architecture. As quantified in Section~\ref{subsec:res-solver-fidelity}, the Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR gap is smaller than the selected direct Hydrostatic FNO and F-FNO RMSEs, whereas the cross-family gaps are comparable to or larger than the direct Boussinesq FNO RMSE. The architecture comparisons below therefore demonstrate the consequences of the reference-aware framework rather than defining its primary contribution.

For three equally weighted reference trajectories, the squared-error-optimal solver-anonymous target is their per-scenario arithmetic mean, $\bar{Y}$. For each scenario, the squared error averaged across references decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{R} \sum_{r = 1} ^ {R}\|f(X) - Y_r\|_2 ^ 2
= \|f(X) - \bar{Y}\|_2 ^ 2
+ \frac{1}{R} \sum_{r = 1} ^ {R}\|Y_r - \bar{Y}\|_2 ^ 2.
\end{equation}
Comparing the centroid with the three references gives a global RMSE of $0.001705$ (95\% bootstrap interval $[0.001665,\, 0.001745]$). The three anonymous checkpoints have equal-reference RMSE $0.002995 \pm 0.000004$ (sample standard deviation across seeds $18$, $36$, and $67$). Applying the decomposition to the mean squared errors across these seeds gives a model-to-centroid RMS term of $0.002462$; this term measures the anonymous model's deviation from the reference centroid. The centroid term quantifies disagreement among the three equally weighted references, while the model-to-centroid term includes surrogate approximation and optimization error. This reference-consensus diagnostic does not identify physical truth.

The corresponding secondary higher-exposure comparison gives conditioned target errors of $0.00209$, $0.00249$, and $0.00287$, respectively, and therefore does not alter the interpretation. Conditioning reduces compromise behavior and preserves solver-specific structure, but it does not identify a physically correct trajectory or universally remove the underlying solver discrepancy.

Figure~\ref{fig:solver-gap-scale} places the same-reference errors of the Hydrostatic FNO, Hydrostatic F-FNO, and Boussinesq FNO alongside the raw solver gaps on one denormalized RMSE scale. The Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR gap is $0.81$ and $0.84$ times the Hydrostatic FNO and F-FNO RMSEs, respectively. The Hydrostatic--Boussinesq and MUSCL-HR--Boussinesq gaps are $1.24$ and $1.01$ times the Boussinesq FNO RMSE.
