\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Unless stated otherwise, model accuracy is measured on the $N = 2500$ held-out test scenarios. MAE, RMSE, and headline relative $L_2$ values are computed after reversing target normalization; max-err remains in normalized target units. Every table uses the same completed common-time evaluation and the checkpoint, dataset, and metric definitions stated in Sections~\ref{sec:methods}--\ref{sec:training}.

\subsection{Common-time contract and numerical evidence}
\label{subsec:res-dataset}

All three main datasets contain $10{,}000$ training, $1{,}000$ validation, and $2{,}500$ test scenarios. Each processed target has shape $50 \times 64 \times 64$, and all $40{,}500$ solver-specific trajectories passed the configured numerical-health checks. The scenario populations match across references: six source families, five bathymetry families, and source strength in $[0.45, 0.85]$.

Table~\ref{tab:solver-validation} summarizes the numerical evidence used to accept the finite-horizon label contract. The categories are grouped by their scientific purpose.

The independent-comparator evidence is deliberately limited. All analytical, verification, and refinement comparisons passed, whereas $11$ of $12$ exploratory descriptive comparisons exceeded their provisional limits. On three production-like cases, trajectory relative $L_2$ against GeoClaw ranged from $0.414$ to $0.537$ for Hydrostatic and from $0.231$ to $0.259$ for MUSCL-HR. These discrepancies remain part of the interpretation: the package supports matched-regime verification, but not pointwise production equivalence or the designation of GeoClaw as physical truth.

The linear weakly dispersive implementation also reproduces its discrete constant-depth phase-speed trend over eight Fourier modes. The maximum relative error is $2.45 \times 10 ^ {-4}$ for the non-dispersive branch and $1.91 \times 10 ^ {-8}$ for $\alpha_B = 1 / 3$, with no conjugate-gradient failures and at most $35$ iterations in the latter branch (Table~\ref{tab:app-bouss-dispersion}). This is a local implementation diagnostic rather than external or event-scale validation.

The central evaluation result is that the remaining reference discrepancy is not small relative to surrogate error. As quantified in Section~\ref{subsec:res-solver-fidelity}, the common-time Hydrostatic--MUSCL-HR RMSE gap is $1.6$ times the Hydrostatic FNO error and $2.1$ times the F-FNO error, while either shallow-water--Boussinesq gap exceeds the same-reference Boussinesq FNO error by more than five times. The architecture comparisons below therefore demonstrate the consequences of the reference-aware protocol rather than defining its primary contribution.

\paragraph{Paired scenario uncertainty.}
The paired scenario bootstrap gives mean per-scenario relative $L_2$ values of $0.222$ ($95\%$ interval $[0.218, 0.226]$) for F-FNO, $0.272$ ($[0.266, 0.278]$) for ConvLSTM, and $0.304$ ($[0.298, 0.310]$) for FNO. Relative to FNO, the paired mean differences are $-0.082$ ($[-0.085, -0.079]$) for F-FNO and $-0.032$ ($[-0.036, -0.028]$) for ConvLSTM. Their per-scenario errors are lower than FNO on $93.2\%$ and $64.0\%$ of the test scenarios, respectively. These intervals describe variation over the fixed test scenarios for the selected checkpoints; they are not multi-seed significance claims.

Relative to the base Hydrostatic FNO, F-FNO reduces the global relative $L_2$ by approximately $22\%$ while using about $7.6\%$ as many parameters. ConvLSTM reduces the same error by about $14\%$ and has the lowest final-frame error among the three models shown in Table~\ref{tab:perframe}. These results are consistent with benefits from axis-factorized global mixing and explicit temporal recurrence, respectively, but the direct architecture table uses one selected seed per configuration. The ranking should therefore be read as a controlled comparison of these runs, not as a population-level significance claim across architectures.

\subsection{Ensemble uncertainty and seed stability}
\label{subsec:res-uncertainty}

The raw seven-member ensemble is under-dispersed. On the ordinary test split, raw coverage is $0.419$, $0.654$, $0.742$, and $0.798$ for nominal $50\%$, $80\%$, $90\%$, and $95\%$ intervals. The error--uncertainty correlation is $0.562$, indicating useful but only moderate spatial ranking.

A validation-only variance multiplier $\gamma = 3.981$ (standard-deviation scale $1.995$) raises test $90\%$ coverage from $0.742$ to $0.920$. It also improves the multi-Gaussian subgroup from $0.664$ to $0.888$ and trench from $0.748$ to $0.924$, while the high-strength tail remains under-covered at $0.821$.

Across the seven individual seeds, the mean per-scenario relative $L_2$ ranges from $0.288$ to $0.306$, with member mean $0.297$ and standard deviation $0.0074$. This is evidence of stability for the Hydrostatic FNO ensemble only; it is not a replicated multi-architecture matrix.

Variance inflation improves interval scale but does not make the ensemble uniformly calibrated. The corrected test coverage is close to nominal at $90\%$, while the high-strength subgroup remains almost ten percentage points below nominal. Together with the moderate error--uncertainty correlation, this supports using ensemble spread as a relative warning signal within the benchmark rather than as a calibrated hazard probability.
